📚 STOP 5 · THE WORLD OF THE BOOK · PUT TWO SOURCES TOGETHER

This is the last step, and it is the one real researchers spend their lives on. Two good sources on the same subject will not say quite the same thing. That is not a problem to be fixed. It is information. Read two pages about the same topic, then ask three questions. Where do they agree? Where do they differ? And the important one: why might they differ? Often the answer is purpose and audience. A page written for children picks different details than one written for scholars. A library that owns Shakespeare's actual printed books knows things a general encyclopedia does not, because it is looking at the objects. A page written a hundred years ago may repeat something later evidence overturned. None of that makes either page a liar. It means every writer chooses, and a careful reader asks what each one chose and why. You have done this all year with characters, weighing Gareth against Launcelot, Robin against the Sheriff. Do it now with two writers.

🎭 STOP 6 · SCENE READING · PLAY THE HIDDEN LISTENER (graded)

In Lesson 6.09, The Hidden Listener, you read two scenes in Shakespeare’s own words rather than Lamb’s retelling: Beatrice hidden in the garden while Hero and Ursula bait her, and Prospero standing unseen while Ferdinand and Miranda speak. Both plant a character where the speakers cannot see them. One is a trap. One is a father watching.

Pick one of those two scenes. Answer five short questions about how the staging works, then record yourself reading it. You read every part, so give each character something different in your voice. You may perform it with other people instead if you would rather.

You have room to breathe on this one. This is the only item in the module you may finish any time before the module closes, so if today is full, do the reading now and record later in the week. Submit through the Canvas assignment. [Teacher: link the Canvas assignment]
