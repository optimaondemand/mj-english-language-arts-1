📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
Plan "What Lasts"
The full plan for your year-capstone comparative essay
This is capstone week. You write the comparative essay that looks back across the whole year, and first you plan it. A board lets you see the whole essay at once and move pieces around before you commit them to Word. You will lay it out in order: your theme and the claim it makes, then your three characters from three texts in turn, and finally what lasts. When the order looks right, writing it is mostly typing.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
In what order will your essay move from your theme to what lasts, and what specific moment does each of your three characters bring?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place and reorder notes. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your "What Lasts" board from VR 6.2B with your theme and three characters chosen, and your project file.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a board and lay out five labeled spots in a line, in this order: (1) your theme and the claim it makes (what this theme reveals about what lasts); (2) Character 1 / Text 1; (3) Character 2 / Text 2; (4) Character 3 / Text 3; (5) what lasts (what all three share, and why this idea keeps coming back).
2
Drop your evidence from VR 6.2B into spots 2, 3, and 4. For each character, place the one specific moment that shows your theme, and under it add one phrase explaining how that moment shows it. A moment is something that happens. A trait is just a word, so push past words to moments.
3
Between the characters, add a small transition note (likewise, in contrast, similarly, while) so the board shows how you move from one character to the next. These transitions go straight into your essay.
4
Fill the what lasts spot. Write one sentence that begins "What all three of these characters share is …" and one sentence on why this theme is worth carrying forward. Then walk the line once and reorder anything out of place, so your claim is clear up front and the ending says what the comparison means rather than repeating the claim.
5
Take a screenshot of your planned essay. Submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 6.3A: Plan the Essay, then use it as your outline. Next you record the "What I Carry Forward" reflection in VR 6.3B and write the final essay in Word. (No headset? Lay the same plan out on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My plan has all five spots in order: theme/claim, three characters from three texts, and what lasts.
☐
Each character has one specific moment with a phrase explaining what it shows, and I added transitions between them.
☐
I submitted a screenshot of my plan to VR 6.3A.
