Lesson 6.03
The Choice to Forgive
Literature · Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The biggest idea in a story is often shown by one hard choice.
Ferdinand and Miranda fall in love, and Prospero gives them his blessing. Then comes the moment the whole tale has been building toward: Prospero faces the men who betrayed him. The big idea a story explores is its theme. Today you trace how Lamb shows the theme of forgiveness through Prospero's single choice.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what theme is and state the theme of forgiveness in The Tempest.
2
I will be able to point to the moment Prospero chooses forgiveness over revenge, and explain what tips him toward it.
⚡ WARM-UP · CHECK IN FIRST
When does forgiving someone take more strength than getting even? (No wrong answer. Just check in.)
📚 THE LESSON

A theme is the big idea a story explores, the truth it shows through its events. The Tempest spends its whole length getting Prospero to one choice, and that choice reveals its theme. First, Lamb softens the ground: Ferdinand passes Prospero's tests, and Prospero gladly gives Miranda to him, which shows a man learning to give rather than only to control. Then the enemies are brought low. Ariel reports that the king and Antonio are truly sorry, and he adds that he, "though a spirit," pities them. That line is the turning point. Prospero answers that if a spirit can feel pity, then he, "a human being like themselves," must have even more compassion. So he forgives Antonio and the king. Notice that the spirit's mercy shames the man into greater mercy. The theme is not stated in a single sentence; Lamb shows it by having Prospero lay down both his anger and, at the very end, his magic. When Prospero buries his books and frees Ariel, the tale ties its two ideas together: letting go of the grudge and letting go of power are the same act of freedom.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · WORD WORK
Find the sentence where Antonio's penitence is called sincere. Penitence means real sorrow for having done wrong. Copy that sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal, then rewrite it one of three ways: swap in a word that means almost the same thing (a synonym), stretch it with more detail, or keep its shape but write about a time someone was sorry. Add one line: what changed, and why.
📖 FROM THE TALE
Open the full tale of The Tempest again (in the drop-down on this week's first lesson) and reread the ending, from Ariel's report that the king and Antonio are sorry through Prospero freeing Ariel and burying his magic. In the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal, copy the exact sentence where Ariel says he pities them "though a spirit," and write one sentence on how that line changes Prospero's mind.
