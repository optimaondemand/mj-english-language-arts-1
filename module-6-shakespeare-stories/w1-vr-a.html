📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
Tales from Shakespeare · "The Tempest"
Charles & Mary Lamb · the prose retelling · read from your own copy
Think of VR here as a giant whiteboard you can walk around in. It is a tool for thinking, the way a word processor is a tool for writing. Today you open a blank board and brainstorm the big ideas in "The Tempest": Prospero's choice between revenge and forgiveness, and the question of freedom that runs through Ariel, Caliban, and Prospero himself. This is the last module of the year, and the ideas you pull out here feed your year capstone, the comparative essay and reflection called "What Lasts."
You are not visiting a ready-made world. You are building a board of your own ideas, using moments you found in the reading.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
In "The Tempest," what does Prospero choose between, and what does the tale say lasts: revenge, or forgiveness and freedom?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place notes and simple objects. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your own copy of the tale and your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up for the module), open to your Week 1 notes.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank board (or clear space) in your VR app. Title it The Tempest: Big Ideas. Across the top, place three idea notes: Revenge, Forgiveness, and Freedom.
2
Under Revenge and Forgiveness, place three to four moments from the tale, one note each: Antonio and the king of Naples setting Prospero and baby Miranda adrift; Prospero raising the storm but promising "no person in the ship shall receive any hurt"; the harpy accusing the men of their cruelty; Antonio weeping and asking forgiveness; Prospero forgiving them both and embracing his brother. Use the moments you actually noticed.
3
Under Freedom, place two or three moments: Ariel longing for liberty and earning it; Caliban kept at slave's labor; Prospero burying his magic books and wand to lay down his power. Notice who ends up free and who does not.
4
Add one answer note at the bottom: in one sentence, say which the tale shows lasts longer, revenge or forgiveness, and what that has to do with freedom. This is your first claim, and you can change it later.
5
Take a screenshot of your finished board. You'll submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 6.1A: Brainstorm Board. (No headset? Build the same board on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board has the three idea notes (Revenge, Forgiveness, Freedom) with real moments from "The Tempest" under them.
☐
