Lesson 6.32
Three Characters, Three Texts
Literature · Week 3 · Year Capstone
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A theme is only as strong as the evidence you can point to.
Yesterday you chose a theme. Today you find the proof. Your capstone essay must trace your theme across at least three characters from at least three different texts you read this year. The rule matters: three different books, not three characters from the same story. For each character you need one specific moment, not a general trait. Today you pick your three and gather the moment for each.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to choose three characters from three different texts who all show my theme.
2
I will be able to name one specific moment, not a general trait, as evidence for each character.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
The theme sentence you wrote in Lesson 6.31, in your Reading Journal. (No new reading this week.)
▶
Your own copies of the year's books, to confirm each moment before you use it.
⚡ WARM-UP · CHECK IN FIRST
When you remember a character, what comes to mind first? (No wrong answer. This shows you where your evidence will come from.)
📚 THE LESSON

Good evidence is specific. If your theme is about forgiveness, "Prospero is forgiving" is not evidence. It is a label. The evidence is the moment: Prospero has the king and his treacherous brother Antonio at his mercy in The Tempest, and instead of taking revenge he forgives them and seals the peace with his daughter's marriage. That moment proves the theme because you can point to exactly when and where it happens. Choosing your three characters works the same way. You need a character who shows your theme, a different text for each one, and a single moment in each you could describe to someone who has not read the book. Spread across at least three texts on purpose, because a comparison across three different stories shows a theme truly lasts, while three characters from one book only shows it matters in that book. As you choose, look for variety, not sameness. Three characters who show your theme in slightly different ways will give you more to write about than three who show it identically. Pick your three, find one real moment for each, and write each moment down as a full sentence in your journal.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · WORD WORK
For one of your characters, write the evidence as a sentence naming the exact moment. Then rewrite it once, replacing any vague verb (like "shows" or "is") with a precise action verb (like "forgives," "refuses," "surrenders"). Add one line: which verb makes the moment clearer?
