📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
Start Your "What Lasts" Theme Board
A board you keep and grow toward the year capstone
In VR 6.1A you brainstormed one tale. Now you open a second board you will keep all module long: your "What Lasts" theme board. A board is a tool for organizing an argument, the same way a chart is. Here you begin collecting the themes that have come back again and again across the whole year's reading: heroism, self-sacrifice, happiness, and duty. By Week 3 this board helps you pick one theme and three characters from three texts for your "What Lasts" essay.
Keep this board. It grows each week and becomes the backbone of your capstone essay.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Which big ideas have kept coming back across this year's books, and where does "The Tempest" fit among them?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can set up labeled columns of notes. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your brainstorm board from VR 6.1A, your Reading Journal, and a quick list of the books you read across the year.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a new blank board. Title it What Lasts. Set up four theme columns across the top: Heroism, Self-Sacrifice, Happiness, and Duty. These are the four themes that have run through the year.
2
Place a note from "The Tempest" under at least two of the columns. For example, Prospero giving up his magic and freeing Ariel could go under Self-Sacrifice; choosing to forgive instead of rule by power could go under Heroism or Duty. Write the character and the moment, not just the title.
3
Now reach back across the whole year. Add at least three more notes from earlier texts to the columns where they fit, naming the character and one moment (for example, a moment of courage from St. George, King Arthur, or Robin Hood; a sacrifice from Daniel or Lancelot). Use the characters you remember best.
4
Look at which column is filling up fastest. Put a small star on the theme you find most interesting so far. You are not committing yet, but this is the start of choosing the one theme your essay will argue.
5
Take a screenshot of your board so far. You'll submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 6.1B: Start the Board. (No headset? Build the same chart on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board has the four theme columns: Heroism, Self-Sacrifice, Happiness, Duty.
☐
It has notes from "The Tempest" and at least three more from earlier texts, each naming a character and a moment.
☐
I starred the theme I find most interesting and submitted a screenshot to VR 6.1B.
