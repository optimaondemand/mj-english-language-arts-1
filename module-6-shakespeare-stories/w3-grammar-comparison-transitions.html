Lesson 6.34
Transitions, the Periodic Sentence, and a Cleaner Draft
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 3
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The right small word tells the reader exactly how two ideas connect.
A comparison essay lives or dies on its connections. Today you learn two tools your capstone needs: transitions that signal whether ideas are alike or different, and the periodic sentence, which saves its main point for the end so a closing line lands. Then you revise: a quick pass to cut clutter and vary how your sentences begin.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to choose a transition that correctly signals likeness or difference between two ideas.
2
I will be able to write a periodic sentence that holds its main point until the end.
⚡ WARM-UP
Read these two: "Both forgive." versus "Likewise, Daniel forgives." Which feels clearer to you in an essay? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

A transition is a word or phrase that shows the reader how two ideas connect. In a comparison, the most useful transitions fall into three groups. To signal that two ideas are alike, use likewise, similarly, or in the same way. To signal a difference, use in contrast, however, whereas, or unlike. To signal a result, use therefore or as a result. Choosing the right one tells your reader exactly what you mean, so they never have to guess. The second tool is sentence shape. A periodic sentence holds its main point until the very end, building up first and landing last: "Across three different stories, told in three different centuries, by three different authors, the same truth keeps returning." The opposite, a loose sentence, states its point first. Periodic sentences are powerful for a closing line, because the reader feels the idea arrive. Once your draft has clear transitions and a strong final sentence, do one revision pass: read each sentence aloud in your head, cut words that add nothing, and make sure your sentences do not all begin the same way. That last pass is where good writing becomes clear writing.

To signal…Use a transition like…Example
Likenesslikewise, similarly, in the same way"Likewise, Prospero chooses mercy."
Differencein contrast, however, whereas, unlike"In contrast, Midas learns his lesson too late."
Resulttherefore, as a result"Therefore, the theme holds across all three."
📊 LOOSE VERSUS PERIODIC · FLIP THE CARD
LOOSE (point comes first)
"The same truth keeps returning across three stories from three centuries by three authors."
Click to see the periodic version
PERIODIC (point comes last)
"Across three stories, from three centuries, by three different authors, the same truth keeps returning." The main point lands on the final words, so the closing feels earned.
