Professor Lovelore
Here is my first question. Robin tells Worman, "But you yourself have shown no mercy, though mercy has been shown to you." What does that tell you about the two of them?
Your answer
Professor Lovelore
Next question. Robin has the frightened Bishop at his mercy, yet he asks him to dinner and sends him on his way in peace. What does that mainly show?
Your answer
Professor Lovelore
Last question. Robin and George-a-Greene trade blows for an hour, and neither wins. Then Robin says, "I have met my match." What does that mainly show?
Your answer
Professor Lovelore

Well done. Look at how each answer sets one person beside another. Worman and Robin each hold the upper hand, and they use it in opposite ways. The Bishop’s fear stands beside Robin’s courtesy. George stands beside Robin as an equal.

A character set beside another so that the contrast shows is called a foil, and it is the word for this reading.
