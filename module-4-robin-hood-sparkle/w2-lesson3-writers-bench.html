Simple sentences, compound sentences, and run-ons
Professor Lovelore At the board

An AI-generated character and voice.
Simple sentence
one complete thought
Robin drew his bow.
Compound sentence
two complete thoughts, joined by a comma and a joining word
Robin drew his bow, and the arrow flew.
and · but · or · so · for · nor · yet
1. No join, run-on
Robin drew his bow the arrow flew.
2. A comma alone is not enough
Robin drew his bow, the arrow flew.
still a run-on
3. Fix a run-on
add a comma and a joining word
Robin drew his bow, and the arrow flew.
or make two sentences
Robin drew his bow. The arrow flew.

✏️ Grammar: Joining Two Thoughts
Step 1 of 3 this week: learn the join.
KindWhat it hasExample
simpleone complete thoughtRobin drew his bow.
compoundtwo complete thoughts, joined by a comma and a joining wordRobin drew his bow, and the arrow flew.
run-ontwo thoughts jammed together, with no join or only a commaRobin drew his bow the arrow flew.
🎯

Maya’s draft
6th Grade Scholar

The water below is loud and cold, and the bridge is only one flat tree trunk. I should be angry. The stranger called me a coward, and my fingers still itch to draw the bow. I will not hide behind my arrows. But I know the fight would not be fair, because he has only a staff. I slip the arrow back into my quiver, and I lay my weapons on the bank. Six feet of ground oak feels straight and true in my hands. He is smiling, so I find myself smiling too. Then his staff swings, my feet leave the bridge, and the stream swallows me whole. I come up coughing, grab a hawthorn branch, and laugh out loud. I lost, yet I am not sorry. A man who beats me fairly is a man I want beside me. I shake the water from my hair, and I ask him to dine with me.
