🔤 Words: The Beggars
Five words from Chapter 12. Learn them before you read.

Say the meaning first, then tap the card to check.

🎭
pretence
tap to flip and hear it
pretence
A false show meant to make others believe something untrue. Robin unties the three young men’s ropes while making a pretence of tying them tighter.
🥣
beggar
tap to flip and hear it
beggar
A poor person who asks others for money or food. A beggar in three old hats strides along the road, singing merrily.
⚔️
adversary
tap to flip and hear it
adversary
An opponent; the person you fight or compete against. Little John goes hunting for his late adversary.
😤
insolent
tap to flip and hear it
insolent
Rude and disrespectful, especially toward someone owed respect. Robin calls the first beggar an insolent rogue.
🧐
presumption
tap to flip and hear it
presumption
Boldness that goes past what is proper or allowed. The beggar says he punished Little John for his presumption.

Professor Lovelore
Next question. In the crowd outside Nottingham, Robin shouts louder than anyone, but his words are against the three young men. What does that mainly show?
Your answer
Professor Lovelore
Last question. At the end, Robin says that as a beggar he won three men’s lives, while Little John counts three hundred pounds in gold. What does that mainly show?
Your answer
Professor Lovelore

Well done. Look at what your three answers share. Little John loses a round and still bets he will win the day. Robin shouts against the young men so that no one guesses who he is. Robin counts three people worth more than three hundred pounds. In each case, the writer lets you know a person through what he says, does, and chooses.

That is called characterization, and it is the word for this chapter.

📖 Read like a Reader, Read like a Writer, Mimic
One sentence from the chapter, three ways.
“He was strangely dressed in ragged clothes but so many folds of these that they would have kept out any weather; his hat was of the same kind – three old hats stuffed into each other and stuck together – and round his neck hung a great leather bag.”Green, Chapter 12
📖 Read like a Reader

This is how Green first shows you the beggar. What kind of man do you picture, and what in the sentence makes you picture him?

✏️ Read like a Writer

Green lists the clothes, then the hat, then the bag, one detail after another. What does the list add that the words “a beggar in rags” would not?

🖊️ Mimic

Write your own sentence on the same pattern, introducing a person by what he or she wears or carries.
