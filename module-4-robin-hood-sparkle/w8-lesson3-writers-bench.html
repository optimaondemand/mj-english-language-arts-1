✅ Correct model
Then Robin bent once more over his horse's neck, clapped spurs into its side, and rode on into the morning.

Three actions share one subject, Robin, so Green names him once and joins the actions: bent, clapped, and rode. Commas separate the three, and and comes before the last. This is combining at work.

Words spellcheck missesMeaningsTwo-second test
its / it’sbelongs to it / it isSay “it is.” If it fits, write it’s.
their / there / they’rebelongs to them / a place / they areSay “they are.” A place? There holds the word here.
to / too / twotoward or before a verb / also or too much / the numberDoes also fit? Write too. A number? Write two.
affect / effectto change something / the resultDoes the fit before it? Write effect.
then / thannext in time / used to compareComparing two things? Write than.

Ruth’s draft
6th Grade Scholar

When I started the book I believed a hero always obeys the law. I thought an outlaw must be a criminal. The two words could never go together. Now I believe the law and what is right are not always the same thing. What changed my mind was Chapter 21, when Robin says, “we were not outlawed lawfully.” Prince John’s mandate made Robin an outlaw and took his lands, so the law was used against an honest man. Then came the end of the book. In Chapter 24, Robin is very ill, and Little John begs for revenge on the nunnery. Robin refuses. He will not hurt a woman, not even the Prioress Little John blames. That showed me a hero keeps his own rules, even when anger would be easy and he is very ill. I still wonder one thing: how can anyone know a law is unjust before it is too late? Green never says.
