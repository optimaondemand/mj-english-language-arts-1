Combine
Robin robs the rich. He helps the poor.→Robin robs the rich and helps the poor.
Vary the opening
Robin flees. Robin hides. Robin sends Marian away.→Pursued by King John, Robin flees, hides, and sends Marian to safety.
The two-second test
Say it is in its place. Does it fit?→If it fits, write it’s. If not, write its.

Three lenses for your draft
  1. Specific. A named scene and its chapter, so a reader can see what changed your mind. Vague words are flagged.
  2. Honest voice. Every I, me, and my is marked. Words that sound like someone else wrote them are flagged.
  3. Variety. How each sentence begins and how long it runs. Repeated openings and runs of short sentences are flagged.
150 to 200 words · what you believed when you started the book, what you believe now, what changed it, and one open question

Your final essay

Revise it in Word, using your teacher’s comments on your first draft. This page holds the checklist and the steps. It does not collect the essay.

one Word .docx · 700 to 900 words
1Check the essay against the list
700 to 900 words, with a clear, arguable thesis that answers the question: What makes a true hero, and what happens when justice and law are not the same thing?
Each of the three body paragraphs has a topic sentence, evidence (a quotation with a signal phrase or a citation), an explanation, and a transition.
A real concession states the strongest opposing view and answers it.
The conclusion restates the thesis in fresh words, addresses the concession, and reaches beyond the book.
Verbs stay in the present tense, at least two sentence-variety tools are used, and at least two quotations are introduced and cited correctly.
The title is Heading 1, the body is Times New Roman 12 or Calibri 11, and Track Changes shows my revisions.
