📚 GRAMMAR · PROPER & COMMON NOUNS
A quick reminder: a common noun names any member of a group and stays lowercase. A proper noun names one specific person, place, or thing and takes a capital letter. Try the three checks below.
⚡ TRY IT · SORT THE NOUNS
Answer each row. Pick one and you'll get instant feedback.
1. Which sentence capitalizes the proper noun correctly?
2. Which of these words is a proper noun and must be capitalized?
3. Which sentence is capitalized correctly?
📚 VOCABULARY · WORDS OF AN UNJUST WORLD
Flip each card to check yourself on this week's words from the Prologue and Chapters 1–2.
⚡ CHECK YOURSELF · WORD FLIP
Read each clue and say the word out loud (or in your head) before you flip. Then check yourself.
A poor peasant bound to work a lord's land, with almost no rights of his own. (Say the word, then tap to flip)
serf. The Saxon poor are treated as mere serfs by their Norman lords.
The crime of betraying your king or country. (Say the word, then tap to flip)
treason. Robin is falsely accused of treason, though he was always loyal to King Richard.
Someone who seizes power that is not rightfully theirs. (Say the word, then tap to flip)
usurper. Green calls Prince John a usurper because he grabs the throne while Richard lives.
The armed servants and followers who serve and protect a lord. (Say the word, then tap to flip)
retainers. The Sheriff keeps armed retainers to enforce his will.
A lord or king a person owes loyalty to. (Say the word, then tap to flip)
liege. Robin's true liege lord is King Richard, even while the king is away.
The forest, especially as a free and living place. (Say the word, then tap to flip)
greenwood. Robin finds refuge in the greenwood once the law turns against him.
