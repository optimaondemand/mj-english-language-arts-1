📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
Finish the Court, Then Say the Verdict Aloud
The final Forest Court and your spoken argument
This is project week. You finish the Forest Court you started in VR 4.7B so it matches your final thesis, then you stand in it and deliver your verdict aloud. Speaking your argument tests whether it holds together, the same way reading it aloud would. Your recording is the spoken version of your essay: state your thesis, walk the for and against stations, answer the other side, and close with your verdict on the essential question. The three screenshots and the recording you make here submit together with your essay as the module project.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Said aloud as you walk your court, does your argument actually prove your verdict on whether Robin is a hero or an outlaw?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset, or any voice recorder if VR is unavailable
Activity type: solo. No live session, no scheduled time, no classmates present.
Tool you'll use: your built Forest Court space, plus a screenshot tool and a voice recorder. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your Forest Court from VR 4.7B and your final essay thesis.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open your Forest Court. Tidy it to match your final thesis: update the center thesis note, make sure each evidence object is labeled with its chapter and page, and confirm the for, against, answer, and verdict pieces are all in place and readable.
2
Capture three screenshots, named as in the module project: a wide shot of the whole court (ForestCourt_Wide_[FirstName]), the center Robin object (ForestCourt_Robin_[FirstName]), and your thesis or verdict object (ForestCourt_Thesis_[FirstName]).
3
Rehearse once aloud, walking the court. Then make a voice or screen recording (3 to 4 minutes) in which you (a) state your thesis; (b) walk the for station and explain each object; (c) name the against station fairly and give your answer; (d) deliver your verdict and connect it to the essential question about a true hero and about justice versus law.
4
Listen back once. Note the one spot that sounded weakest out loud and fix it in your final essay before you submit. The spoken version often finds the gap a written draft hides.
5
Save your three named screenshots and your recording. Submit them with your essay in the Module Project assignment. The completion check for this VR step is VR 4.8A: Finish the Forest Court. (No headset? Submit your labeled court sketch from VR 4.7B plus the recording narrating your argument for full credit.)
