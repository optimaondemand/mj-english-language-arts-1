📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
The Adventures of Robin Hood
Roger Lancelyn Green · Chapters 3–5 · read from your own copy
VR is your walk-around whiteboard again, the same thinking tool. This week you gather what Chapters 3–5 show about how Robin gathers a band and begins to live by a code: a hundred men in Lincoln green, the rescue of Will Scarlet, the quarter-staff duel that turns a stranger into Little John. You are testing whether a man called an outlaw acts more like a leader. Everything you collect feeds your essay "Portrait of a Hero".
You are not visiting a ready-made world. You are building a board of your own evidence, using details you found in the reading.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
As Robin gathers his band, does he behave like a criminal gang leader or like a just leader with a code?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place notes and simple objects. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your own copy of the book and your Reading Journal, open to your Week 2 notes.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank board (or clear space) and title it Week 2: The Band and the Code. This is a fresh brainstorm space, separate from your main evidence board.
2
Make a note for the question this week tests: when Robin gathers his men, does he act like a gang leader or like a just leader? Write it in one sentence.
3
Make four to six evidence notes, one detail each, from Chapters 3–5: Robin gathers a hundred valiant men "all clad in Lincoln green"; Brother Michael openly praises Robin and is banished for it; the Sheriff's men extort "tithes" from Robin's old tenants; Robin and the band rescue Will Scarlet when he is seized; Robin meets a tall stranger on the log bridge, loses the quarter-staff duel, then laughs and welcomes him as Little John. Use the details you actually noticed.
4
Add one or two label notes marking each detail as evidence Robin is a hero, an outlaw, or that the law acts unjustly. For example, losing a fair fight and then welcoming his opponent shows fairness and good humor, not a criminal's pride.
5
Take a screenshot of your finished board. You'll submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 4.2A: Brainstorm Board. (No headset? Build the same board on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board names the question this week tests and has four to six real details from Chapters 3–5.
☐
I labeled each detail as evidence Robin is a hero, an outlaw, or that the law acts unjustly.
☐
I submitted a screenshot of my board to VR 4.2A.
