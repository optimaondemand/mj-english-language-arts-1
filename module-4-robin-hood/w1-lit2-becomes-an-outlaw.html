Lesson 4.02
Outlawed on a False Charge
Literature · Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A reader can know the truth even while the characters do not.
Today Earl Robert Fitzooth is declared an outlaw, but you, the reader, know he is innocent. When the audience knows something the characters on the page do not, that gap is called dramatic irony. Watch how Green uses it. We watch a good man condemned by people who are lying, and that makes the injustice land harder.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what dramatic irony is and find it in Robin's outlawing.
2
I will be able to describe how the charge against Robin is false and who profits from it.

⚡ WARM-UP · WHERE DO YOU STAND?
If a court punishes an innocent person on purpose, how unjust is that? Slide to your gut feeling. (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

Dramatic irony happens when you, the reader, know something that the characters do not. Green builds Chapter 2 around it. On the eve of his wedding to Marian Fitzwalter, Earl Robert Fitzooth is shown as a man "of pity and of determination," about thirty years old, who has spent his wealth helping poor serfs. You know he is honest. But Prince John and the Sheriff slip into his feast disguised as palmers, and his own steward, the steward Worman, betrays him. They charge Robin with treason, but the charge is false. His real "crimes" are sheltering the families of accused men and selling his lands to help the poor. He is declared an outlaw and his lands are seized. The detail that gives the game away is who benefits: Prince John wanted Robin's wide lands all along. This is the moment the title points to. The most just man in the story is made, by law, a criminal, and the law-versus-justice question you will argue all module long is now fully loaded. Your job today is to name exactly where the dramatic irony lives.

📖 NOW READ
Read Chapter 2, "How Robert of Locksley Became an Outlaw." As you read, find the moment Robin is officially declared an outlaw, and note what the charge says versus what he actually did. Record it in your Reading Journal.

⚡ THINK, THEN REVEAL
Write your own answer first, then open the panel to compare. There is no auto-grading here. The thinking is the point.
In one or two sentences: what is the difference between what Robin is charged with and what he actually did? Why does that gap matter?
Reveal a strong response
Robin is charged with treason against the prince, but in truth he was loyal to the real king, Richard, and his only "offenses" were sheltering hunted families and spending his lands to help the poor. The gap matters because it shows the charge is a lie used to steal his property. The law calls him a traitor while he is the most just man in the story. That is dramatic irony: you know the truth the court pretends not to.
