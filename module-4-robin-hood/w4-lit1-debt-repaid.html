Lesson 4.19
The Debt Repaid: Honest Dealing Among Outlaws
Literature · Week 4
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Sometimes a result is the opposite of what the powerful intended.
In Chapter 9 Sir Richard returns to repay the four hundred pounds Robin lent him, with interest. On the same day, the greedy abbey's own monk is robbed of a matching sum, so the abbey unknowingly funds the repayment. When a result twists against what a character intended, the craft term is irony. Watch how Green uses it to score a point about who really deals fairly.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain the irony in how Sir Richard's debt gets repaid.
2
I will be able to show how the repaid loan supports the idea that Robin's band deals honestly.

🏛️ A LITTLE BACKGROUND FIRST
Last week Robin lent Sir Richard four hundred pounds to save his lands from the greedy abbot. This week the loan comes due. At the same time, the men who hold real legal power in the story stay the same: Prince John, who rules while King Richard is away, and the cruel Sheriff of Nottingham, who enforces the Forest Laws. These are the people the story sets against Robin. Keep them in view, because the next two lessons put the Sheriff at the center.
📚 THE LESSON

Irony is a gap between what is expected and what actually happens. In Chapter 9 the gap is sharp. Sir Richard rides back to Sherwood and repays the four hundred pounds Robin lent him, and he adds a gift of yew bows and arrows on top. That alone proves the loan was an act of trust, not theft, because honest men repay honest debts. Then Green adds a second turn. On the very same day, Robin's men happen to rob a monk of the same abbey of a matching sum. So the abbey, which tried to seize Sir Richard's land over the debt, ends up paying the debt without knowing it. That is the irony: the greedy abbey funds the very repayment it tried to profit from. The point lands without a speech. Among the so-called outlaws, a debt is honored and trust is rewarded, while the legally powerful are out-maneuvered by their own greed. Hold this beside the chapters to come, where the Sheriff keeps trying to trap Robin and keeps being fooled.
