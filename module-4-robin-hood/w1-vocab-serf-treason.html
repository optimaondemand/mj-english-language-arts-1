Lesson 4.05
Words of an Unjust World
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Learn the words of a world, and the world opens up to you.
This week's words come from the Prologue and Chapters 1–2: the ranks of people in a divided England and the crimes the powerful invent. You'll meet them where they live in the text. Reading a word inside a real sentence lets you use the surrounding words to figure out its meaning. The craft term for those surrounding hints is context clues. Then you'll make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
Some of this week's words name kinds of people; others name crimes or wrongs. Which feels harder to pin down? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below belongs to the opening of The Adventures of Robin Hood. Some name where a person stands in this divided society (the bound serf, the free yeoman, a lord's armed retainers). Others name the wrongs that drive the story: betraying a king, seizing a throne, wearing the colors of a cruel master. Watch for them in the reading.

WordWhat it means here
serfa poor peasant bound to work a lord's land, with almost no rights of his own
yeomana free man who farms his own small land, ranking above a serf but below a noble
treasonthe crime of betraying your king or country (the false charge laid against Robin)
usurpersomeone who seizes power that is not rightfully theirs (Green calls Prince John a usurper)
retainersthe armed servants and followers who serve and protect a lord
liverythe uniform or colors that show whose servant or soldier a man is (the Sheriff's livery)
liegea lord or king a person owes loyalty to (Robin's true liege lord is King Richard)
greenwoodthe forest, especially as a free and living place; here, Robin's refuge
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Week-1 Reading Journal lesson):
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside the Prologue or Chapters 1–2. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.
⚡ TRY IT · THE RIGHT WORD
Choose the word that fits each sentence from the world of the legend.
1. The Saxon poor are treated as mere ______, bound to till the land for their Norman lords.
2. Robin is falsely accused of ______ against the prince, though he was always loyal to King Richard.
3. Green calls Prince John a ______ because he takes a throne that is not rightfully his.
