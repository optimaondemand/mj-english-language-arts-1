Lesson 4.22
Keeping Your Verb Tenses Consistent
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 4
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Pick a tense and stay in it, unless the meaning truly changes.
You'll learn to keep one verb tense across a sentence and a paragraph, and to spot the jolt a reader feels when the tense slips for no reason. Then a short quiz checks what you've got. This matters most when you write about a book, where one tense, the present, is the standard choice.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to spot a sentence where the verb tense shifts for no reason.
2
I will be able to fix a tense shift, and use one consistent tense when I write about the book.
⚡ WARM-UP
Read this aloud: "Robin enters Nottingham and sold the meat cheaply." Does anything feel off? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

Tense tells the reader when something happens: past ("Robin entered"), present ("Robin enters"), or future ("Robin will enter"). Verb–tense consistency means you stay in one tense unless the meaning truly requires a change. A shift for no reason jars the reader. "Robin enters Nottingham and sold the meat" mixes present and past in one action, so it stumbles; fix it to "Robin enters Nottingham and sells the meat" or "Robin entered Nottingham and sold the meat." A real change in time is allowed: "Robin won the silver arrow yesterday, and today he plans his next trick" is fine, because the two actions happen at different times. The tense changes for a reason. One special rule helps you all module. When you write about a story, use the present tense, even though you read the events as past. We say "Robin fools the Sheriff," not "Robin fooled the Sheriff." This is the standard way to discuss any book, and you will use it in your essay. As you draft this week, pick the present tense, then read your sentences back and make every verb agree in time.

TenseExampleWhen to use it
PastRobin fooled the Sheriff.telling about real events that already happened
PresentRobin fools the Sheriff.discussing what happens in a story (use this in your essay)
Shift to avoidRobin enters and sold the meat.never; the tense jumps for no reason
