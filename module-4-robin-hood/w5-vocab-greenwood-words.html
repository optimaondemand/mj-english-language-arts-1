Lesson 4.29
The Words of Disguise and the Road
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 5
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Learn the words of a world, and the world opens up to you.
This week's words come from Chapters 12 through 14: the tricks of disguise, the people Robin meets on the road, and the gear of a wandering man. You'll meet them where they live in the chapters. Reading a word inside a real sentence lets you use the surrounding words to figure out its meaning. The craft term for those surrounding hints is context clues. Then you'll make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
Robin and his men keep using disguise to get what they want. What word would you pick for a clever, harmless trick? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below belongs to Chapters 12 through 14. Some name the band's tricks (a disguise, a jest). Some name the people Robin meets on the road (a beggar, a tanner, a minstrel, a bridegroom). Others name the gear and places of a wandering life (a quarter-staff, the butts, a palfrey). Watch for them in the chapters.

WordWhat it means here
disguisea changed appearance that hides who you really are (Little John dresses as a beggar; Robin as a stranger)
jesta joke or playful trick (the band beards the Sheriff partly in jest)
beggara poor person who asks others for money or food (the disguise Little John chooses)
tannera worker who turns animal hides into leather (Arthur-a-Bland's trade)
minstrela traveling singer and musician of the Middle Ages (Allin-a-Dale)
quarter-staffa long, stout wooden pole used as a two-handed weapon (Robin and the tanner fight with these)
bridegrooma man on his wedding day; the man being married (the rich old knight Allin's love is forced toward)
buttsthe targets, or the practice ground, used for archery
palfreya gentle riding horse, not a war horse (the everyday mount of travelers)
yeomana free man who farms his own small land, ranking below a knight but above a serf
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc in your OneDrive ELA6 folder):
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside Chapters 12 through 14. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.
