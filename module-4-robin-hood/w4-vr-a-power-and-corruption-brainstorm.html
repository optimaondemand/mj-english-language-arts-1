📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
The Adventures of Robin Hood
Roger Lancelyn Green · Chapters 9–11 · read from your own copy
VR is your walk-around whiteboard, the same thinking tool. This week the camera turns to the other side: the Sheriff of Nottingham, Prince John, and the Abbot, the people who hold legal power. You gather what Chapters 9–11 show about how that power is used, and how Robin meets it. A hero is partly defined by the villain he stands against, so this evidence sharpens your essay "Portrait of a Hero".
You are not visiting a ready-made world. You are building a board of your own evidence, using details you found in the reading.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
When the people with legal power are the corrupt ones, what does that say about calling Robin an "outlaw"?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place notes and simple objects. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your own copy of the book and your Reading Journal, open to your Week 4 notes.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank board (or clear space) and title it Week 4: Power and Corruption. This is a fresh brainstorm space, separate from your main evidence board.
2
Make a note for the question this week tests: if the men with legal power are the corrupt ones, how fair is the word "outlaw"? Write it in one sentence.
3
Make four to six evidence notes, one detail each, from Chapters 9–11: Sir Richard returns and repays the loan honestly, with bows and arrows added as interest; Prince John sets the silver-arrow contest as bait, and Robin walks into his enemies' midst in disguise and wins; Robin trades places with a butcher, sells meat cheaply, and outwits the Sheriff in his own town; the greedy Abbot's own money unwittingly funds the repayment. Use the details you actually noticed.
4
Add one or two label notes marking each detail as evidence Robin is a hero, an outlaw, or that the law acts unjustly. For example, the Sheriff being fooled in his own town shows the powerful using the law for themselves, while honest debts are kept among the "outlaws."
5
Take a screenshot of your finished board. You'll submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 4.4A: Brainstorm Board. (No headset? Build the same board on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board names the question this week tests and has four to six real details from Chapters 9–11.
☐

☐
