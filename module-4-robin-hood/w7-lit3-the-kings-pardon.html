📚 THE LESSON

A theme is the central idea a whole story explores, bigger than any single scene. Across this book Green has built one idea steadily: the law and justice are not always the same thing. Robin was named an outlaw by a law Prince John bent for his own gain, yet Robin acted more justly than the men who carried the law's authority. In Chapter 21 the disguised King Richard, the rightful ruler, lets Robin capture the Bishop of Hereford, then reveals himself. Robin and his men kneel, and the king pardons Robin and the whole band and gives back the earldom Prince John seized. For the first time, the law and justice agree. This is the legend's great reconciliation, and it is the single strongest piece of evidence for reading Robin as a hero: when true authority finally looks honestly at him, it declares the outlaw just. Use this chapter to put your theme into one sentence.
