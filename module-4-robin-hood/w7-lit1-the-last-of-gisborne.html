Lesson 4.37
The Climax: When Robin Faces His Enemy
Literature · Week 7
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every long story builds to one moment the whole plot has been leaning toward.
The danger that has been gathering for weeks finally breaks. Robin meets Sir Guy of Gisborne in a fight to the death, and grief over Will Scarlet pushes Robin to an act that complicates the hero reading. The moment a story's central conflict comes to a head is its climax. Today you find the climax and weigh what it shows about Robin.

I will be able to explain what a climax is and point to it in these chapters.
2
I will be able to weigh one moment here as evidence for or against calling Robin a hero.

📚 THE LESSON

The climax is the turning point a plot has been building toward, the moment the central conflict finally breaks open. For six weeks the danger has gathered. The Sheriff, the Bishop, the traitor Worman, and Sir Guy of Gisborne have all closed in on Robin. In Chapter 19 the conflict between Robin and Gisborne, set up all the way back in Week 1, comes to a head. The two men meet, fight, and Robin kills Gisborne. That is the climax of the whole book. But Green does not let the moment feel like a clean victory. Around this turning point Will Scarlet is killed, and Robin, in grief and rage, shoots fifteen verderers. The same chapter that defeats the villain also shows Robin at his most dangerous. Your job today is to hold both: the climax that proves Robin can win, and the violence that complicates calling him a simple hero.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · WORD WORK
Find one sentence in Chapter 18 or 19 that uses palmer or infidel. Copy it into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal, then rewrite it one of three ways: swap in a word that means almost the same thing (a synonym), stretch the sentence with more detail, or keep its shape but write about something in your own life. Add one line: what changed, and why.
📖 NOW READ
Read Chapters 18–19. Chapter 18 is a quieter, stranger chapter (the affair of the witch of Paplewick) that slows the pace before the storm. Chapter 19 is the climax: Robin and Gisborne. As you read, mark the exact sentence where you think the book's central conflict turns. Note it in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal.
