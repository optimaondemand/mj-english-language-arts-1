Lesson 4.17
The Words of the Greenwood
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 3
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Learn the words of a world, and the world opens up to you.
This week's words come from Chapters 6 through 8: the people of the forest world and the favors and weapons they trade. You'll meet each word where it lives in the chapters. Reading a word inside a real sentence lets you use the surrounding words to work out its meaning. The craft term for those surrounding hints is context clues. Then you'll make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's greenwood words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
When you hit a word you do not know while reading, what do you usually do first? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below belongs to Chapters 6 through 8. Some name people of the medieval world (the yeoman, the abbot, the hermit). Others name a favor asked, a weapon swung, or the forest itself. Watch for them as you read.

WordWhat it means here
yeomana free man who farms his own small land; not a noble, but not a serf either
abbotthe head of a monastery; here, a wealthy and greedy churchman
boona favor that someone asks for or grants ("I beg one last boon of you")
quarter-staffa long wooden pole used as a weapon, gripped with both hands
hermita person who lives alone, often for religious reasons (Friar Tuck lives as a hermit by the river)
hermitagethe dwelling where a hermit lives, such as a small cell by the riverside
greenwoodthe green forest, especially as the outlaws' home and refuge
retainera servant or follower who is kept by a lord or household
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal:
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside Chapters 6 through 8. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.
⚡ TRY IT · THE RIGHT WORD
Choose the word that fits each sentence from the world of the greenwood.
1. "I beg one last ______ of you," said the knight before riding away.
2. Robin spares the honest ______ who works his own small field.
3. Friar Tuck lived as a ______ in a small cell by the riverside.
4. She could ply the ______ as well as any man in the band.
