📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
The Adventures of Robin Hood
Roger Lancelyn Green · Prologue and Chapters 1–2 · read from your own copy
Think of VR here as a giant whiteboard you can walk around in. It is a tool for thinking, the way a word processor is a tool for writing. Today you start a workspace you will keep all module long. You will gather what the Prologue and Chapters 1–2 actually show about how Robin becomes an outlaw and whether the law that named him one was just. Everything you collect feeds your module project, the analytical essay "Portrait of a Hero", which asks: is Robin a hero or an outlaw?
You are not visiting a ready-made world. You are building a board of your own evidence, using details you found in the reading.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why is Robin declared an outlaw, and does the law that names him one act justly?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place notes and simple objects. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your own copy of the book and your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Reading Journal lesson), open to your Week 1 notes.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank board (or clear space) in your VR app. Title it Hero or Outlaw? This is your workspace for the whole module.
2
Make a note for the question this week tests: was Robin made an outlaw justly, or was the law itself unjust? Write it in one sentence so you can answer it with evidence below.
3
Make four to six evidence notes, one detail each, from the Prologue and Chapters 1–2: Robin is born in the greenwood and charged to "bring help to the down-trodden"; Prince John seizes power and accuses wealthy men of treason to take their lands; the Sheriff blinds and burns out poor men under the Forest Laws; the miller Much is about to be blinded for killing a deer to feed his children; the steward Worman betrays Robin; Robin is declared an outlaw on false evidence and his lands are seized. Use the details you actually noticed.
4
Add one or two label notes that mark each detail: does it make Robin look like a hero, or like an outlaw, or does it show the law acting unjustly? For example, being framed on false evidence shows the law is the problem, not Robin. These labels become your first sort for the essay.
5
Take a screenshot of your finished board. You'll submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 4.1A: Brainstorm Board. (No headset? Build the same board on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board names the question this week tests and has four to six real details from the Prologue and Chapters 1–2.
☐
I labeled each detail as evidence Robin is a hero, an outlaw, or that the law acts unjustly.
☐
I submitted a screenshot of my board to VR 4.1A.
