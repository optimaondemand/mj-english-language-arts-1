📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
The Adventures of Robin Hood
Roger Lancelyn Green · Chapters 6–8 · read from your own copy
VR is your walk-around whiteboard, the same thinking tool. This week you gather what Chapters 6–8 show about the Merry Men and the community Robin serves: his rule for whom to rob, the loan to Sir Richard of Legh, Marian joining the band, and Friar Tuck. You are weighing whether the "outlaw" actually protects the honest people around him. Everything you collect feeds your essay "Portrait of a Hero".
You are not visiting a ready-made world. You are building a board of your own evidence, using details you found in the reading.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
When Robin sets a rule for whom to rob and uses what he takes to help the poor, is that justice or theft?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place notes and simple objects. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your own copy of the book and your Reading Journal, open to your Week 3 notes.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank board (or clear space) and title it Week 3: The Merry Men and the Community. This is a fresh brainstorm space, separate from your main evidence board.
2
Make a note for the question this week tests: when Robin robs by a rule and gives to the poor, is that justice or theft? Write it in one sentence.
3
Make four to six evidence notes, one detail each, from Chapters 6–8: Robin's rule to waylay "some bold baron, bishop or abbot" but to hinder no honest yeoman or labouring man, and never a company with a woman in it; Little John bringing the sorrowful Sir Richard of Legh to "dinner"; Robin lending Sir Richard the money to save his lands from the greedy Abbot; Marian outshooting the foresters and joining the band as a fighter in her own right; Robin seeking out and recruiting the good Friar Tuck. Use the details you actually noticed.
4
Add one or two label notes marking each detail as evidence Robin is a hero, an outlaw, or that the law acts unjustly. For example, robbing only the corrupt rich by a fixed rule shows a code of justice, not random crime.
5
Take a screenshot of your finished board. You'll submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 4.3A: Brainstorm Board. (No headset? Build the same board on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board names the question this week tests and has four to six real details from Chapters 6–8.
☐
I labeled each detail as evidence Robin is a hero, an outlaw, or that the law acts unjustly.
☐
I submitted a screenshot of my board to VR 4.3A.
