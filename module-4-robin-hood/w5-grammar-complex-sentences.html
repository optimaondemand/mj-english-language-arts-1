Lesson 4.28
Complex Sentences: Joining a Big Idea to a Smaller One
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 5
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A complex sentence shows which idea leans on which.
You'll learn to build a complex sentence by joining an idea that can stand alone to one that cannot. This is the tool you need for your essay, because it lets you connect a reason to a claim in one clean sentence. Then a short quiz checks what you've got.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to tell an independent clause from a dependent clause and join them into a complex sentence.
2
I will be able to place the comma correctly when the dependent clause comes first.
⚡ WARM-UP
Read this: "Because Robin judged the marriage unfair." Does it feel finished to you? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

A clause is a group of words with its own subject and verb. An independent clause can stand on its own as a sentence: "Robin stopped the wedding." A dependent clause has a subject and verb too, but a starter word like because, when, although, since, or after leaves it unable to stand alone: "because the match was unfair." A complex sentence joins one of each. "Robin stopped the wedding because the match was unfair." The dependent clause leans on the independent one, and the starter word shows how the two ideas connect (a cause, a time, a contrast). The comma rule is short. If the dependent clause comes first, put a comma after it: "Because the match was unfair, Robin stopped the wedding." If the independent clause comes first, you usually need no comma. This is the sentence you will reach for in your essay, where you constantly tie a reason to a claim.
