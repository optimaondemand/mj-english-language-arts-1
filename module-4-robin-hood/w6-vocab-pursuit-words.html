The Words of Pursuit and Peril
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 6
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
As the danger rises, so does the vocabulary of danger.
This week's words come from Chapters 15 through 17, where the mood darkens: words for being hunted, held, and betrayed. You'll meet them where they live in the chapters. Reading a word inside a real sentence lets you use the surrounding words to figure out its meaning. The craft term for those surrounding hints is context clues. Then you'll make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.

📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below belongs to Chapters 15 through 17. Some name the danger closing in (an ambush, a sworn enemy, a tempest). Some name the people of power Robin faces (a bishop, a verderer, a pinner). Others name betrayal and capture (a traitor, a ransom). Watch for them in the chapters.

WordWhat it means here
ransommoney paid, or demanded, to free a captive (Robin holds the Bishop for ransom)
ambusha surprise attack from a hidden position (the Bishop sets one for Robin)
traitora person who betrays a trust or a side (Worman, once Robin's steward)
bishopa high-ranking church leader who oversees many churches (the Bishop of Peterborough)
verdereran officer who guarded the royal forest and enforced its harsh laws
pinnera town officer who rounded up and held stray animals (George-a-Greene)
tempesta violent storm with strong wind and rain (the storm the band shelters from)
swornbound by an oath; here, vowed as a fixed enemy (Sir Guy is Robin's sworn enemy)
perilserious and immediate danger (the peril Robin is in as his enemies close in)
routto defeat and scatter an enemy force completely (the archers rout the Bishop's party)
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc in your OneDrive ELA6 folder):
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside Chapters 15 through 17. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.
