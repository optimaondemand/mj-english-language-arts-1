Lesson 4.38
The Stranger in Black: Who Is Really Watching?
Literature · Week 7
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A writer can tell you something important while seeming to hold it back.
At the tournament a mysterious Black Knight appears and no one is told who he is. Green is giving you a clue while withholding the name. A hint planted early that something larger is coming is called foreshadowing. Today you track how Green prepares the return of the rightful king.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what foreshadowing is and find it in Chapter 20.
2
I will be able to explain why the king's return matters to the question of whether Robin is a hero or an outlaw.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
The Adventures of Robin Hood, Chapter 20, "The Silver Bugle and the Black Knight." (Read from your own copy of the book.)
▶
Your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in Week 1, kept in your OneDrive ELA6 folder)
⚡ WARM-UP · CHECK IN FIRST
A powerful stranger shows up in disguise and watches before acting. Why might someone do that? (No wrong answer. Just check in.)
📚 THE LESSON

Foreshadowing is a hint an author plants early so that a later event feels prepared rather than random. At the tournament at Ashby-de-la-Zouche a jousting stranger appears, the Black Knight, and Green deliberately does not name him. The mystery is the clue. A nameless, masterful knight who commands respect and moves quietly among the people is, in this legend, the returned King Richard the Lionheart, the rightful ruler the band has waited for since Week 1. Robin wins a silver bugle here, but the real event is hidden in plain sight: the king is back, and he is watching. This matters to your essay. Robin has been called an outlaw by Prince John's law. If the rightful king sees Robin and judges him just, then the legal label and the real moral truth are about to split apart in front of everyone. Track the clues Green gives you about who the Black Knight is.

Add one line: which clue in the sentence points to a king?
📖 NOW READ
Read Chapter 20. As you read, hunt for two clues that the Black Knight is someone important, even though Green does not say his name. Mark them in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal.

⚡ CHECK YOURSELF · CLUE OR NOT?
Each card names a detail from Chapter 20. Predict whether it is a clue that the Black Knight is the king, then tap to flip and check.
The stranger is a master at arms whom no one can match. (Tap to flip)
Clue. A knight better than every famous fighter is exactly what we would expect of a great king returning in secret.
Green refuses to give the Black Knight a name. (Tap to flip)
Clue. Withholding the name on purpose is foreshadowing. The secrecy itself signals that the identity is a big reveal.
Robin wins a silver bugle at the tournament. (Tap to flip)
Not the main clue. The bugle is Robin's prize for his own skill. It is a nice detail, but it does not point to who the Black Knight is.
