📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
Put Robin on Trial in a Space You Build
The first version of your Forest Court composition
A "Forest Court" is a space you assemble where Robin's case as hero-or-outlaw is laid out, the way a court lays out the evidence for and against. This is not a place to tour. You build it yourself out of simple objects and notes: a center for Robin's case, a side for the evidence that he is a hero, a side for the evidence that he is an outlaw, and your verdict. You are turning the flat plan from VR 4.7A into an argument you can walk around. This is the first version. You finish and narrate it next week, and it submits with your module project.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If someone walked through the court you build, would the arrangement of objects make your argument clear before you said a word?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: an open space or board where you can place simple objects and label them with notes. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your essay plan from VR 4.7A and your finished evidence board, with your position and the other side's best point marked.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
In an open space, build the center of the court: place one object to stand for Robin on trial, and label it with your thesis (your claim about whether he is a hero or an outlaw). Everything else in the court points back to this.
2
Build a "for" station on one side and an "against" station on the other. At the for station, place one object for each of your three body-paragraph pieces of evidence, each labeled with its chapter and page. At the against station, place one object for the other side's best point.
3
Next to the against station, add a small answer note: your one-sentence reply to that point. The for and against stations together are your essay's evidence and concession, built into the room.
4
Place a verdict object at the front of the court with a note stating your final answer to the essential question. Then look at the whole arrangement: the size and position of objects should make your argument readable, with your strongest evidence closest to the center.
5
Take a screenshot of your whole court. Submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 4.7B: Build the Forest Court. Next week you tidy it to match your final thesis, capture the three named screenshots, and record your walkthrough for the module project. (No headset? Sketch the same court layout on paper or in a slide, labeling each station, and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My court has a center object for Robin labeled with my thesis, with everything pointing back to it.
☐
I built a "for" station with three labeled evidence objects and an "against" station with the other side's best point and my answer.
☐
I placed a verdict object and submitted a screenshot of the whole court to VR 4.7B.
