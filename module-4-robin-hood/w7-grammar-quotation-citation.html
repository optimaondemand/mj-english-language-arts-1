Lesson 4.40
Putting Evidence on the Page the Right Way
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 7
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A quotation only helps your argument if you introduce it and cite it correctly.
Your essay needs evidence from the book. Today you learn to introduce a quote with a signal phrase, punctuate it correctly, and cite the page so a reader can check it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to introduce a quotation with a signal phrase and punctuate it correctly.
2
I will be able to add a parenthetical citation so a reader can find the quote in the book.
⚡ WARM-UP
A friend tells you "the book says Robin was kind" but cannot show you where. How convinced are you? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

A quotation is the book's exact words, set inside quotation marks. To use one well, do three things. First, introduce it with a signal phrase that names who is speaking or acting, so the quote does not drop in from nowhere: Green writes, "…" or As Robin says, "…". Put a comma after the signal phrase and start the quote with a capital letter. Second, punctuate the quotation marks correctly: in American style, commas and periods go inside the closing quotation mark. Third, add a parenthetical citation, the page number in parentheses after the closing quotation mark, with the sentence's period after the parenthesis: Green writes, "…" (142). The citation lets a reader open the same book and find your evidence. A "dropped quote," one with no signal phrase and no citation, makes your essay look unsupported even when you are right. As you draft this week, every quote gets a signal phrase, correct punctuation, and a page number.
