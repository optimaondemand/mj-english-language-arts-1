Lesson 4.11
Words of the Greenwood
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 2
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Learn the words of a world, and the world opens up to you.
This week's words come from Chapters 3–5: the life of an outlaw band and the loyalties that bind it. You'll meet them where they live in the text. Reading a word inside a real sentence lets you use the surrounding words to figure out its meaning. The craft term for those surrounding hints is context clues. Then you'll make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's greenwood words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
The word "outlaw" can sound like a villain or like a hero, depending on who says it. Which way do you lean so far? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below belongs to Chapters 3–5. Some name the outlaw life (the greenwood, the quarter-staff, the man placed outside the law). Others name the loyalties and burdens of this world: the lord you owe service to, the harsh tenth taken from the poor. Watch for them in the reading.

WordWhat it means here
outlawa person placed outside the protection of the law; here, Robin and his band
greenwoodthe forest as a free and living place; the band's home in Sherwood
quarter-staffa long, stout wooden pole used as a fighting weapon, gripped with both hands
liegea lord or king a person owes loyalty to (Robin's true liege lord is King Richard)
tithea tenth part taken as a payment or fine, often a harsh burden on the poor
valiantbrave and determined (the "hundred valiant men" who join Robin)
renounceto give up or formally reject someone or something (Lord Fitzwalter renounces Robin)
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Week-1 Reading Journal lesson):
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside Chapters 3–5. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.
⚡ TRY IT · THE RIGHT WORD
Choose the word that fits each sentence from the world of the legend.

2. On the narrow bridge, Robin and the stranger fight with the ______.
3. The Sheriff's men demand a heavy ______, a tenth of all the poor tenants own.
