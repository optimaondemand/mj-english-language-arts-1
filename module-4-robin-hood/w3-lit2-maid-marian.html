Marian Joins the Band: A Heroine Who Acts
Literature · Week 3
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A writer can define a character by what she can do, not by what is done to her.
In Chapter 7 Maid Marian comes to Sherwood in disguise and outshoots the foresters at a festival. She is no prize to be rescued. She can fence, handle a quarter-staff, and shoot a longbow. Watch how Green builds her through action. When a writer shows a character through what she does rather than telling you about her, that is indirect characterization.

1
I will be able to explain how Green uses indirect characterization to show Marian's skill and courage.
2
I will be able to point to one action in Chapter 7 that proves Marian belongs in the band by merit.

📚 THE LESSON

There are two ways a writer can build a character. Direct characterization tells you outright: "Marian was brave." Indirect characterization shows you through action, speech, and choices, and lets you draw the conclusion yourself. Chapter 7 leans hard on the indirect kind. Marian arrives at the Gamwell festival disguised as a shepherdess and outshoots the trained foresters. Green also tells you she can fence with a sword, ply the quarter-staff, and shoot the longbow. He does not stop at calling her admirable. He shows her winning. This matters for your reading of the whole book. The band Robin gathers is a community joined by skill and loyalty, not by birth or rank, and Marian earns her place the same way Little John did, by proving herself. As you read, gather Marian's actions like evidence. Each one is a brick in the case that she is a hero in her own right.

📊 TWO WAYS TO BUILD A CHARACTER
Direct (the writer tells)Indirect (the writer shows)
"Marian was a skilled archer."Disguised as a shepherdess, Marian outshoots the trained foresters.
"Marian was bold."She walks into a festival watched by her enemies and competes anyway.
