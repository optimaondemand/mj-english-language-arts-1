Lesson 4.03
What Kind of Man Is Robin Hood?
Literature · Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A writer shows you who a person is through what they do, not just what they are called.
You have met Robin twice now: the hidden archer who saves Much, and the earl condemned on a lie. Today you put those scenes together to build a picture of his character. The way an author reveals what a person is like, through actions, words, and choices, is called characterization. By the end you will be able to back up a claim about Robin with a moment from the text.

📚 THE LESSON

Characterization is how an author shows you what a person is like. There are two ways to do it. Sometimes the author tells you directly, calling Robin a man "of pity and of determination." More often the author shows you through action and lets you judge for yourself. Look at what Robin has already done. He kept a hidden watch over Sherwood and struck down the man who would have blinded Much, so he is merciful and protective. He spent his own wealth on the poor and sheltered hunted families, so he is generous and brave enough to take a risk for others. After the betrayal, he does not beg or surrender; he takes to the greenwood, so he is determined and refuses to bow to an unjust order. Young Much and Will Scarlet already speak of him with loyalty and hope, which tells you how the common people see him. Each of these is a trait backed by a moment, and that pairing, a claim plus the moment that proves it, is exactly what your module essay will need. Today you start collecting those pairs.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · START YOUR CHART
In the "Week-by-Week Notes" section, make a two-column chart titled Hero or Outlaw? On the left, list reasons Robin looks like a hero (merciful, generous, loyal followers). On the right, list anything that could make him look like an outlaw in the law's eyes (he is officially declared a criminal; he will rob travelers). Add at least one moment under each heading from this week's reading.
