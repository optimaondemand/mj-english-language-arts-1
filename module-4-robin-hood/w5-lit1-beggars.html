📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A story often tests a second character to show what its hero stands for.
Today Robin stays home and Little John takes the road. He puts on a beggar's rags to walk straight into the Sheriff's Nottingham. Watch what John does with Robin's rules when Robin is not there. When a story gives a supporting character their own chapter to act and choose, the craft term is characterization: the methods an author uses to show what a person is like.

1
I will be able to explain what characterization is and name one method Green uses to reveal Little John.
2
I will be able to point to one moment in Chapter 12 that shows whether Little John follows Robin's code of selective, principled robbery.

📚 THE LESSON

Characterization is how an author shows you what a person is like. A writer can do it directly, by telling you a trait, or indirectly, by showing you what a character says, does, and chooses. In this chapter Green hands Little John the spotlight. Jealous that Robin gets all the daring exploits, John disguises himself as a beggar and goes to "beard" the Sheriff in his own town. Pay attention to his choices, because they are evidence. Whom does John target, and whom does he leave alone? Does he rob anyone honest and poor, or does he aim at the powerful and the corrupt? Robin's code, set earlier in the book, was to strike at the rich and greedy and to spare honest working people. This chapter asks whether John keeps that rule when no one is watching him. Notice too how the comedy works. John walks into the lion's den, fools the Sheriff's men, and walks out, and his nerve and wit mirror Robin's. Your job today is to track one moment that reveals who Little John really is. Keep the essay question in the back of your mind: is this band heroic, or just a gang of robbers?
