📚 GRAMMAR · APPOSITIVES
A quick reminder: an appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun right next to it, usually set off by one or two commas. Rate each statement below.
⚡ CHECK YOURSELF · TRUE OR NOT?
For each statement, choose how true it is. Pick one and you'll get instant feedback.
1. "An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun right next to it."
2. "An appositive at the end of a sentence needs two commas, one before it and one after it."
3. "An appositive always appears immediately after the noun it renames, and moving it elsewhere never changes the sentence's clarity."
