📚 GRAMMAR · VERB–TENSE CONSISTENCY
A quick reminder: pick one tense and stay in it unless the meaning truly changes. When you write about a book, use the present tense. Write your own answer first, then reveal.
⚡ THINK, THEN REVEAL
Write your own answer first, then open the panel to compare. There is no auto-grading here. The thinking is the point.
Fix this sentence so both verbs agree in tense: "The Sheriff schemes and lost the bargain."
Reveal a strong fix
Best fix: "The Sheriff schemes and loses the bargain" (or, entirely in the past, "schemed… lost"). Both verbs must share one tense unless the meaning truly changes.
In one sentence, explain why writers use the present tense to discuss what happens in a book, even though the events happened "in the past" of the story.
Reveal a strong answer
This is the standard way to discuss any book: we say "Robin fools the Sheriff," not "fooled," because writing about a story uses the present tense, even though the reader experiences it as past events.
Is this tense shift allowed, and why: "Robin won the arrow yesterday, and today he plans his next trick"?
Reveal a strong answer
Allowed. The two actions truly happen at different times (yesterday and today), so the tense change reflects a real change in time. That's different from a shift for no reason.
