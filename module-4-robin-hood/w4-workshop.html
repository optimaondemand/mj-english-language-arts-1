📚 STOP 5 · THE WORLD OF THE BOOK · FIND THE CENTRAL IDEA

You can find a source, skim it, and read its features. Now the hardest one. Every informational article is built around a central idea, the single thing the whole page is trying to tell you. It is not the topic. "Castles" is a topic. "Castles changed shape as weapons got better" is a central idea, because it makes a point about the topic. Find it by reading the opening section and the closing one, then asking yourself: if I could keep one sentence from this page, which would it be? Then look at what holds it up. Informational writers support a central idea in a few predictable ways: with facts, with definitions of terms you would not know, with examples, and with numbers. Name which ones your article uses. This is the same skill you are using on Green's novel every week, where you find a theme and then gather the moments that prove it. Nonfiction just says its main point out loud instead of hiding it in a story.
