📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
Plan "Portrait of a Hero"
The full plan for your analytical essay
This week's chapters bring the legend's turning point: Robin kills Sir Guy of Gisborne, and the returned King Richard pardons Robin and restores his earldom (Chapters 18 to 21). The rightful king decides the outlaw was just all along, which is your strongest evidence for the hero reading. Now you plan the essay. A board lets you see the whole essay at once and move pieces around before you commit them to Word. You will lay it out in order: your claim, your evidence for your position, the evidence against, your answer to that evidence, and your verdict. When the order looks right, writing it is mostly typing.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
In what order will your essay move from your claim to your verdict, and where does the other side's best evidence go?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place and reorder notes. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your finished evidence board with your position note (from VR 4.6B) and your project file.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a board and lay out five labeled spots in a line, in this order: (1) your claim about whether Robin is a hero or an outlaw; (2) your evidence for that claim; (3) the evidence against it (the other side's best point); (4) your answer to that evidence; (5) your verdict, what your reading finally says about a true hero and about justice versus law.
2
Drop your strongest evidence from your board into the evidence-for spot. Place an early, a middle, and a late moment from the book, so your evidence stretches across the whole story. Under each, add one phrase explaining what it shows.
3
Fill the evidence-against and answer spots. Name the other side's strongest point fairly (for example, that Robin does kill and rob), then write your answer for why your position still holds. This is the move that turns a list into an argument.
4
Walk the line once and reorder anything out of place. Make sure your claim is clear up front, your strongest evidence is not buried, and your verdict says what your reading means rather than just repeating the claim.
5
Take a screenshot of your planned essay. Submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 4.7A: Plan the Essay, then use it as your outline for the draft. Later this week you build the Forest Court in VR 4.7B from this same plan. (No headset? Lay the same plan out on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My plan has all five spots in order: claim, evidence for, evidence against, answer, and verdict.
☐
My evidence-for spot has an early, a middle, and a late moment, each with a phrase explaining what it shows.
☐
I named the other side fairly, wrote my answer, and submitted a screenshot to VR 4.7A.
