📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
When a story repeats a pattern on purpose, the pattern itself is telling you something.
Today you meet Little John on the narrow log bridge, and you see Robin's band rescue Will Scarlet. Watch how Robin makes a friend: he meets a stranger, they fight as equals, and the stranger joins him. A detail or event a story repeats on purpose is called a motif. By the end you will be able to spot this recruiting motif and say what it reveals about Robin.

⚡ WARM-UP · WHERE DO YOU STAND?

📚 THE LESSON

A motif is a detail, image, or event a story repeats on purpose to build meaning. Chapter 5 gives you the most famous version of one. Robin meets a tall stranger on a narrow log bridge over a stream. Neither will step aside, so they fight with quarter-staffs, and the stranger knocks Robin into the water. Instead of sulking, Robin laughs, blows his horn, and welcomes the giant, whose name is John Little, into the band, comically renaming him Little John. This is not a one-time scene. It is the start of a pattern: Robin meets someone worthy, tests him in a fair fight, and recruits him whether Robin wins or loses. When you see a pattern like this repeat, that is a motif, and it reveals something about Robin. He measures a man by courage and skill, not rank, and he would rather gain a strong friend than protect his pride. Earlier in Chapter 4 you see the same loyalty run the other way, when Robin and his men risk themselves to rescue Will Scarlet from the Sheriff's men. Your job today is to name the recruiting motif and track its steps.

📖 NOW READ
Read Chapter 4 and Chapter 5. As you read the bridge scene, track the order of events so you can rebuild it from memory. Note the recruiting pattern in your Reading Journal.
