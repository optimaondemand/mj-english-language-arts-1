Lesson 4.21
Robin and the Butcher: Winning by Wit
Literature · Week 4
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A hero's cleverness can matter as much as his strength.
In Chapter 11 Robin trades places with a butcher, walks into Nottingham, sells meat cheaply, fools the Sheriff into a bad bargain, and slips away. He never draws his bow. A character who beats a stronger enemy through cunning and disguise plays the role of the trickster. Watch how Green uses this comic chapter to round out Robin's characterization.

📚 THE LESSON

So far Robin has won with his bow and his code. Chapter 11 shows a different side of him. He swaps clothes with a butcher, enters Nottingham as an ordinary tradesman, and sells meat so cheaply that he draws a crowd and a curious Sheriff. The Sheriff, smelling a profit, strikes a bargain to buy the stranger's cattle, and Robin leads him a merry chase before slipping away the richer. A character who uses cunning, disguise, and a quick tongue to outwit a stronger opponent is playing the trickster role. This rounds out Robin's characterization. He is not only brave and just, he is clever, and his wit is a weapon. The comic tone matters too. By making the Sheriff look foolish, Green chips away at the antagonist's power. An enemy you can laugh at is an enemy you fear a little less. Hold this beside the earlier chapters. Each escape and each trick adds to the same argument the whole book is building, that the man the law calls an outlaw is more than a match, in courage and in wit, for the men who are supposed to govern him.
