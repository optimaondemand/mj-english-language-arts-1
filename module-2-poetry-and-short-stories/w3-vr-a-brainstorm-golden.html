📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
The Golden Touch
Nathaniel Hawthorne · read the full story in your lesson or your own copy
Use VR here as a giant whiteboard you can walk around in: a tool for thinking, the way a word processor is a tool for writing. Today you brainstorm what Hawthorne's story actually says, then begin forming your own response. The board feeds your module project, the letter "Dear Author, I Disagree."
You are not visiting a ready-made world. You are building a board of your own ideas, using moments from the story.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What is Hawthorne claiming about wealth and happiness, and do you agree?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place notes and simple objects. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: the story and your Reading Journal, open to your Week 3 notes.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank board (or clear space) in your VR app. This is your workspace.
2
Make a note for the author's apparent message. In one sentence, write what Hawthorne seems to be claiming: that the common things within everyone's reach are worth more than the riches people chase, and that love matters far more than gold.
3
Make four or five evidence notes, one moment each, from the story: Midas counting his gold in the dark room; the wish granted by the stranger; the gift turning small at first (the unreadable book, the useless spectacles), then turning serious (he cannot eat or drink); Marygold hardening into a golden statue; the river that undoes the curse; and the old Midas saying the only gold he loves is the tinge in his grandchildren's hair. Use the moments you actually noticed.
4
Add your own response on one or two notes: do you agree that having less brings happiness, partly agree, or disagree? Why? Place these notes next to the moments that made you think that way. This is the start of your side of the letter.
5
Take a screenshot of your finished board. You'll submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 2.3A: Brainstorm Board. (No headset? Build the same board on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board names Hawthorne's apparent message and has four or five real moments from the story.
☐
I added my own response (agree, partly agree, or disagree) with a reason.
☐
I submitted a screenshot of my board to VR 2.3A.
