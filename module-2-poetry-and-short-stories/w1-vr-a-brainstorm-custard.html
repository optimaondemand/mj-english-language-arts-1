🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What is Nash claiming about courage, and do you agree?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place notes and simple objects. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your own copy of the poem and your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Reading Journal lesson), open to your Week 1 notes.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank board (or clear space) in your VR app. This is your workspace.
2
Make a note for the author's apparent message. In one sentence, write what Nash seems to be claiming: that real courage shows in action, not in bragging. The "brave" pets all run or hide, and only Custard, the one everyone calls a coward, actually fights the pirate.
3
Make four or five evidence notes, one detail each, from the poem itself: who is called brave and how they behave when the pirate breaks in (Mustard flees, Ink and Blink hide, Belinda cries for help); what Custard does ("gobbled him, every bit"); the excuses the others make afterward; the repeated line "But Custard cried for a nice safe cage"; the way the ending returns to the beginning so nothing really changes. Use the details you actually noticed.
4
Add your own response on one or two notes: do you agree with Nash's claim, partly agree, or disagree? Why? Place these notes next to the evidence that made you think that way. This is the start of your side of the letter.
5
Take a screenshot of your finished board. (No headset? Build the same board on paper or in a slide.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board names Nash's apparent message and has four or five real details from the poem.
☐
I added my own response (agree, partly agree, or disagree) with a reason.
☐
I have a screenshot of my board.
