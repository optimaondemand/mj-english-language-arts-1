📚 THE LESSON

An adjective is a word that describes a noun. It tells you which one, what kind, or how many. Hawthorne leans on adjectives to make his gold feel real: a burnished cup, a fluted golden pillar, the dark and dreary dungeon, the lustrous stranger. Strip those describing words out and the picture goes flat, so adjectives are part of how a writer controls what you see. The second tool today is the complex sentence. A complete idea that can stand on its own is a clause. A complex sentence joins one clause that can stand alone to a second one that cannot, using a connecting word such as because, when, since, although, or if. For example: "When Midas kissed his daughter, she turned to gold." The first part ("When Midas kissed his daughter") cannot stand alone, but it tells you exactly when the main thing happened. Complex sentences let you show how two ideas connect, which is the heart of clear argument writing, and you will need it for next week's project.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL · THE COMMA RULE
Where does the comma go in a complex sentence? (tap to see)
If the connecting word starts the sentence, put a comma after the first part: "Because his food turned to gold, Midas could not eat." If the main idea comes first, you usually need no comma: "Midas could not eat because his food turned to gold."
