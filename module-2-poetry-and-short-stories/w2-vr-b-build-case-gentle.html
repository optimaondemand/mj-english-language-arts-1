📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
Do Not Go Gentle into That Good Night
Dylan Thomas · building toward your letter
In VR 2.2A you gathered ideas. Now you arrange the strongest version of Thomas's point against your own, then say your argument out loud. Speaking an argument tests whether it holds together. A fair letter states the author's view honestly before answering it, so you will build both sides on a board, then record a short version aloud as a rehearsal for your project letter.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What is Thomas's strongest point, and how do you answer it out loud?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tools you'll use: a blank board for two columns of notes, and a voice recorder. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your brainstorm board from VR 2.2A and your own copy of the poem.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a board and make two columns: "What Thomas is urging" and "What I think."
2
In the first column, write the strongest, fairest version of Thomas's claim, with two notes of evidence from the poem (for example, the repeated "Rage, rage" line, and the four kinds of men who all refuse to give in). State his point at its best.
3
In the second column, write your counter-point (agree, partly agree, or disagree) and two notes that support it. For example, is there strength in peace and acceptance too, or does the personal turn to the dying father change how you read the message?
4
Make a voice recording (1–2 minutes). Out loud: state Thomas's strongest point fairly, give your counter-point, and back it with one moment from the poem. Hearing it aloud shows you where the argument is still thin.
5
Save your recording and take a screenshot of your two-column board. Submit both to the Canvas assignment VR 2.2B: Rehearse Your Case. (No headset? Build the columns on paper or in a slide and record with any voice recorder.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board has two columns: Thomas's strongest point and my own point, each with two pieces of evidence.
☐
My recording (1–2 minutes) states the opposing view fairly before I answer it, with one moment from the poem.
☐
I submitted the screenshot and the recording to VR 2.2B.
