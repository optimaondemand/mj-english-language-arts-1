Lesson 2.11
Words for Strong Feeling, and Making Them Your Own
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 2
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The right word can carry a whole feeling.
You'll own three words this poem depends on (rage, grieve, and figurative) and keep practicing the rewrite move: take a sentence with the word and remake it three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain rage, grieve, and figurative in my own words.
2
I will be able to take a sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP · WHERE DO YOU LAND?
How well do you know the difference between rage and grieve? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

Three words do heavy lifting in this poem, and they're worth owning. Rage means to feel or show violent, uncontrolled anger, the poem's most important verb, the opposite of going "gentle." Grieve means to feel deep sadness, especially over a loss, which is the feeling underneath all that fierceness. And figurative describes language that means something other than its plain, literal sense, the tool that lets "night" stand for death. Notice how rage and grieve sit close together but pull in different directions: one burns hot, one aches. You don't truly own a word until you use it, so keep practicing the same rewrite move from Week 1: take a sentence that contains the word and remake it three ways: swap a near-synonym, stretch it with more detail, or keep the shape and fill it with your own life. Each remake makes you handle the word, and that is how it sticks.

💡 WORD WORK IN YOUR READING JOURNAL
Start with this sentence: "The grieving son begged his father to rage against the end." Rewrite it three ways: (1) swap a near-synonym for grieving or rage; (2) stretch it with more detail about the scene; (3) keep its shape but write about someone you would not want to give up. Add one line: which rewrite taught you the word best, and why?
⚡ CHECK YOURSELF · USING THE WORDS

2. Which word is the closest synonym for rage?
3. Which is a good rewrite of "The son begged his father to fight on" using your own life?
