📚 THE WORLD OF THE BOOK · HOW TO SKIM

Midas is a Greek myth, and the Greek world it comes from is a real place you can go read about. Last module you practiced finding a source you can trust. This week you learn what to do in the first thirty seconds after you open it. You do not read an informational page the way you read a story. You read it twice, and the first pass is a skim. Run your eye down the headings. Read the first sentence of each section, because in informational writing that sentence usually announces what the section will do. Look at the bold words, the pictures, and the captions under them. Then answer one question: does this page actually answer what I asked? If it does not, close it and try another. Skimming is not lazy reading. It is how you avoid spending twenty minutes inside a page that was never going to help you, and it is the difference between a researcher and someone clicking around.

🎤 RECORD IT · "THE WORLD OF THE BOOK" (1–2 MINUTES)
Skim first, then read properly. Then record yourself for one to two minutes, without reading from a page:
☐ Your question
☐ What your thirty-second skim made you expect the page would say
☐ What you actually found once you read it, in your own words
☐ Was your skim right? Where was it wrong?
This one is interesting to listen to because being wrong is allowed. A skim that missed is worth more than one that guessed safely.
