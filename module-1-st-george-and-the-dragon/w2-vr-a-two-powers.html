📖 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
St. George and the Dragon
Michael Lotti, Chapters 5–9
In Chapters 5–9, Marcellus meets two radically different kinds of power making two radically different claims on him. The dragon commands through fear; the Christians draw through something harder to name. You will build two groupings of objects (one for the power of the dragon, one for the power of the Christians) and label both with observations drawn from the text. This visual contrast exercise feeds directly into your writing this week.
🏭 LITERARY & HISTORICAL FRAME
The real risk of being a Christian: around AD 300, in the years the Author's Note places this story, Christians across the empire often met quietly, in private homes and out-of-the-way places, because Emperor Diocletian and Galerius were moving to stamp out the faith. In the book, Agathon's Christians gather in an old shed on Titus's estate, allowed only because Titus tolerates them. That fragile, hidden gathering is the real social reality of Christian life in an empire that had not legalized the faith.
Two worlds in collision: many of the oldest stories turn on a clash between two kinds of world: one ruled by a monster and built on fear and false worship, the other built on community, warmth, and loyalty freely given. Your two groupings are that same contrast, the one the writer renders in prose through word choice, tone, and imagery.
See the contrast before you write it: you train your eye on the contrast before you train your pen on it. What you see in space, the difference between the two groupings, is the raw material for the contrasting language you will write later this week.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What makes the dragon's power feel different from the Christians' power, and how can you show that difference in space?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset
Activity type: solo; you build on your own, at your own pace
Tools you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place objects and notes, with room for two separate groupings, and a writable surface or note tool for your labels. [Teacher: confirm the build/objects/notes tools students use in the current VR platform]
To start: open a blank space. You are not touring a ready-made world; you build both groupings yourself from objects you choose.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank board or space in your VR app. This is your workspace.
2
Build Grouping A: The Dragon's Power. Choose 2–3 objects from your VR app's library that represent the kind of power the dragon holds over those who serve it in Chapters 5–9: Regina, the jewel traders, the bandits, the army officer Cephalus. Think fear, demand, domination, the lure of those red eyes. Arrange them together.
3
Build Grouping B: The Christians' Power. Choose 2–3 different objects to represent the kind of power (or draw) the Christians have: community, prayer, willingness, something Marcellus cannot quite name. Arrange these separately, with visible space between the two groups.
4
Add three signed labels using a writable surface or note tool (or jot them in your M365 Reading Journal and keep a card in frame): one for Grouping A describing the dragon's kind of power; one for Grouping B describing what the Christians' power feels like to an observer; and one placed between the two, written as Marcellus: what does he feel standing between these powers? The Marcellus label must use at least one Week 2 vocabulary word.
5
Take a screenshot of both groupings and the space between them, with all three labels visible. Submit it to the Canvas assignment VR 2.A: The Two Powers.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
