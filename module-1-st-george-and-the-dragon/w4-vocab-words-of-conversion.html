Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 4
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A change of heart has its own vocabulary.
This week's words come from the chapters where Marcellus becomes George: the language of conversion, sorrow, and new beginning. You'll meet them in the story, then make one your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
Several of this week's words name a deep inner change. Which kind of change feels hardest to put into words? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below appears in Chapters 15–19, where Marcellus turns from his old life, is baptized, and receives a new name. Notice how many name a movement of the heart: sorrow, dedication, the rejection of the old self. The writer is using them to show a change you cannot see from the outside.

WordWhat it means here
baptisma Christian rite of initiation by water, symbolizing cleansing and new life
confessiona statement admitting one's sins or wrongdoing
anointto mark someone with oil as a sacred sign
repentto feel sorrow for wrongdoing and turn away from it
sponsora person who stands with and supports another at baptism (Pasikrates, for George)
convictiona firm, deeply held belief
disloyalnot loyal; unfaithful to a person, cause, or duty
humblemodest; not proud; lowly in rank or spirit
cultivateto prepare and care for land or growth; to nurture (the meaning of "George," a farmer)
covetto desire wrongly something that belongs to another
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in your Reading Journal:
  1. Swap: replace the word with a near-synonym. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside Chapters 15–19. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into your journal; one becomes your rewrite sentence above.
⚡ TRY IT · THE RIGHT WORD
Choose the word that fits each sentence from the world of the novel.
