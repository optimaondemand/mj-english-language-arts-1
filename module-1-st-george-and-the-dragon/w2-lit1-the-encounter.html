📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A story turns when something new asks the main character to change.
In Chapters 5–6, a new power reaches for Marcellus. First Regina tells him about a real dragon she worships; then Marcellus rides out and meets it face to face. Watch how the dragon pulls at him through fear, flattery, and those red eyes, and notice that he does not simply give in.

🏛️ A LITTLE HISTORY FIRST
Long before this story, the Greeks and Romans told tales of monsters and of gods who demanded sacrifice. Lotti draws on that tradition: his dragon claims to be more ancient than the gods and asks for offerings of living animals. Notice, though, that the dragon also speaks the language of the empire: loyalty, power, the good of Rome. That is what makes it dangerous. It does not sound like a monster; it sounds like everything Marcellus already believes.
📚 THE LESSON

Every long story has a moment where the ordinary world cracks open and something new asks the main character to change. In Chapters 5–6, that crack opens. The dragon arrives as a power that demands sacrifice and worship. The writer makes it physical and strange: its body is sinewy, its great talons the size of a man's foot, its eyes red and bottomless. Watch how Marcellus reacts. A new call rarely gets an instant yes; more often the main character hesitates, argues, or tests the new power. That hesitation is not weakness in the writing. It is the writer showing you how much is at stake. Notice that Marcellus does not surrender: he draws his knife, asks the dragon hard questions, and even pricks the soft skin under one scale. As you read, find the exact sentence where Marcellus is pulled toward the dragon and pushes back. That push is the story's hinge.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · WORD WORK

Copy it into your journal, then rewrite it one of three ways: swap in a word that means almost the same thing, stretch the sentence with more detail, or keep its shape but write about something in your own life. Add one line: what changed, and why.

⚡ CHECK YOURSELF · READING CLOSELY
For each detail from Chapters 5–6, choose what it mainly shows. Pick one and you'll get instant feedback.
1. Regina worships the dragon and brings it sacrifices, even after it devoured a horse before her slave's eyes.
2. The dragon argues that Christians are a threat to the Empire and asks Marcellus to drive them off his estate.
3. Marcellus does not simply submit. He asks the dragon, "What is the Empire for?" Why does this matter?
