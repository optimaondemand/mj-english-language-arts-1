When Did It Happen? Verbs Carry Time
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 2
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Change a verb's tense and you change when, and sometimes how much it still matters.
You'll review past, present, and present-perfect tense, then see why one of them is a writer's secret tool for showing that the past is still shaping now. A short quiz checks what you've got.

1
I will be able to identify simple past, simple present, and present perfect tense.

⚡ WARM-UP
Which sounds like the danger is still hanging over Marcellus right now? (No wrong answer. Trust your ear.)
📚 THE LESSON

A verb does more than name an action. It carries time. The simple past tells you something happened and is done: Marcellus saw the dragon. The simple present tells you it is happening now, or is always true: Marcellus sees the dragon. The present perfect (has or have plus a past form) tells you something happened in the past but still matters: Marcellus has seen the dragon. That last one is the writer's quiet power tool. When you say the dragon has lived near that cave longer than anyone can remember, the threat is not over. It is still pressing on the present. This is exactly the point about Marcellus's encounters: they are not just events that happened, they are events that have changed him and are still working on him. When you write this week, choose your tense on purpose: use present perfect when you want a reader to feel that the past is still alive.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL · A FORM TO KEEP
present perfect: tap to see how to build it
Present perfect = has/have + past participle. has seen, have endured, has lived. Use it when a past action still has a grip on the present. Test: can you add “and it still matters now”? If yes, present perfect fits.
