📚 STOP 4 · THE WORLD OF THE BOOK · FINDING A SOURCE YOU CAN TRUST

Marcellus lived in a real place. This week you go find out something true about it. Start by making your question small enough to actually answer. "What was Rome like?" is too big to research. "What did a Roman soldier eat?" is a question you can finish. Then decide where to look. An encyclopedia article is a good first stop, because it is written for readers who do not know the subject yet and it gathers what many people have found out. That also makes it a secondary source, which means the writer was not there and is reporting on the work of others. A primary source comes from someone who was there. Both are useful once you know which one you are holding. Before you read a page, judge it: Who published it? Is there a date? Does it say where its information came from? A page with no publisher, no date, and no named sources is not worth your time, no matter how good it looks.

📋 PICK ONE THING TO LEARN ABOUT THE ROMAN WORLD
food · clothing · armor and weapons · roads and travel · houses · money and trade · religion · government · army life · medicine · entertainment
Choose the one you are actually curious about. You will keep learning about this world all module, so pick something you want to know.
💡 WHERE TO LOOK · ALL THREE ARE FREE
▶ Ducksters · Ancient Rome  ducksters.com/history/ancient_rome.php
Start here. Short articles on daily life, food, clothing, family, the army, government, religion, and engineering.
▶ National Geographic Kids · History  kids.nationalgeographic.com/history
Look under World History for Ancient Rome. Strong photographs.
▶ World History Encyclopedia  worldhistory.org
Harder reading, written for adults. Try it if you want more depth, and notice that every article names its author, its date, and the books it drew on.
