📖 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
St. George and the Dragon
Michael Lotti, Chapters 20–21
The image of a champion facing a dragon is one of the most persistent images in Western art. Raphael and Uccello painted it; Lotti wrote it. This week you compose it: arrange objects in space to recreate the climactic moment of Chapter 21, then write your battle paragraph inside the headset, with the composition surrounding you. What you build is an iconographic composition: a spatial image, not an illustration.
🏭 LITERARY & HISTORICAL FRAME
The most painted scene in Western Christian art: Paolo Uccello painted Saint George and the Dragon three times between the 1430s and 1460s; Raphael at least twice (1504 and 1506); Tintoretto, Rubens, and Donatello each made interpretive choices about the same compositional problem: where the dragon sits relative to George, which direction the lance points, how large the horse is, what surrounds them. Every arrangement says something different. You now work in that tradition.
The central combat: in many old stories the heart of the tale is a single great contest: the hero against the creature that stands for a dark, destructive power. Readers have long seen the dragon as a kind of winter: barrenness, death, the force that holds the world in darkness, with the hero's strike as spring breaking through. Your composition is your version of what that looks like.
Writing inside the composition: you write your battle paragraph while your arrangement surrounds you. The spatial experience shapes what you notice and the language you reach for. What the arrangement implies, your prose should make explicit.
🎯 THE WRITING TASK
Write a 5–7 sentence battle paragraph describing George's attack on the dragon, using at least three participial phrases: one at the beginning of a sentence, one in the middle, and one at the end. Check each: does the participial phrase refer to the correct noun?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset
Activity type: solo; you compose and write on your own, at your own pace
Tools you'll use: a blank space, simple objects to arrange, and a writable surface or note tool you can write a paragraph on. [Teacher: confirm the build/objects/notes tools students use in the current VR platform]
To start: open a blank space. You are not touring a ready-made world; you compose the scene yourself from objects you choose. (If your app has no writable surface, write the paragraph in your M365 Reading Journal and keep it in frame for the screenshot.)
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Build your battle composition with 3–6 objects. Arrange objects from your VR app's library to represent the climactic moment of Chapter 21. This is iconography, not illustration; you do not need a literal dragon. Compose the feeling: power, conflict, the direction of force, the moment before or after the blow. Think about which objects are dominant and which are low.
2
Add a signed label naming the specific moment from Chapter 21 you are depicting (one sentence) and one Week 5 vocabulary word that captures its quality.
3
Write your battle paragraph on a writable surface near your composition (5–7 sentences, three participial phrases as described above). Notice whether being inside the space changes how you write.
4
Take a screenshot showing both your composition and your paragraph in one frame. Submit it to the Canvas assignment VR 5.A: Battle Composition, and paste your paragraph text into the Canvas comments box so your teacher can read it clearly.
