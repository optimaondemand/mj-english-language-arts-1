◆ SPEAKING IN ANOTHER PERSON'S VOICE
Compose and perform a speech as a character from the novel
This week you will write and deliver a short speech as if you were a specific character from the book. It is not an impression; it is an act of imagination rooted in knowledge of the character. To speak well as someone, you must understand what they believe, what they have lost, and what they value most. This activity asks you to apply everything you have learned about St. George and the Dragon by speaking as one of its characters at one of its most significant moments.
📖 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
St. George and the Dragon
Michael Lotti, Chapters 22–24
You will record a memorial spoken in a character's voice: a character from the novel speaking in memory of someone they have lost. Use VR as a private workspace to draft, rehearse, and record your speech as a voice recording. Keep that recording, because it also becomes part of your module project. Choose your speaker and subject carefully. The memorial should feel earned, rooted in what you know about both characters and in what the novel says about loss, faith, legacy, and what endures.
🏭 LITERARY & HISTORICAL FRAME
An old and demanding kind of writing: writing a speech in the voice of a named figure is one of the oldest writing exercises in the Western tradition, taught in schools for well over a thousand years. You are given a character and asked to say what that character would say at a specific moment. It was saved for more advanced students because it requires real understanding of the character's world and what drives them. You cannot fake it.
The memorial speech: a speech in memory of someone who has died is one of the oldest forms of public speaking. Lincoln's Gettysburg Address is one famous example. What gives a memorial its weight is specific detail, true feeling, and the discipline not to claim more than the speaker actually knows.
What your speaker knows: the strongest memorial is one where the speaker's specific knowledge of the subject is audible: what only this speaker, from this vantage point, could say. Choose a speaker and subject whose relationship is rich enough to produce specific detail. General grief is thin. Particular love and particular loss are not.
✍ CHOOSE YOUR CHARACTER AND SUBJECT
Choose one speaker and one subject. The speaker is the voice you inhabit; the subject is the person they are memorializing.
A
A Christian witness memorializing George. Someone who saw George's martyrdom and now stands at his grave, speaking of what he was and what his death means to those who remain.
B
George (as himself) memorializing the dragon. The dragon was the adversary, but also a creature, and George faced it. This is the most demanding option: it requires understanding both characters deeply and walking the line between acknowledging the dragon's reality and keeping the novel's moral framework.
C
A Roman soldier who witnessed the martyrdom, memorializing Marcellus. Someone who knew Marcellus before his conversion, who served alongside him, and now stands after his execution trying to understand what changed him and what he died for.
📝 PREPARE YOUR SPEECH BEFORE YOU ENTER VR
Draft your speech in your M365 Reading Journal before entering VR. You will speak from these notes in the headset; the recording is a live performance, not a read-aloud. Aim for 2–3 minutes. Your memorial should address:
  • Who is being remembered and what they meant to the speaker
  • One specific moment from the novel that the speaker witnessed or remembers
  • What the speaker believes the person died for, or what they left behind
  • What endures: the legacy, the example, what cannot be taken by death or empire
Use at least two Week 6 vocabulary words naturally. Legacy, testimony, veneration, immortal, and martyr are especially fitting for a memorial.
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset
Activity type: solo; you set up, draft, rehearse, and record on your own, at your own pace
Tools you'll use: a blank space, one or two simple objects, a writable surface or note tool for one label, and a voice recorder. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
To start: open a blank space. You are not touring a ready-made world; you set up your own quiet spot to draft, rehearse, and record in.
📝 WHAT TO DO IN THE HEADSET
1
Orient and place one object. Find a place that feels right for your memorial. Place one object that represents the person being remembered, symbolically, not literally; it can be an object or a light, anything the speaker can address. Add a signed label naming who is being remembered.
2
Record your memorial. Stand facing the object you placed and make a voice recording (2–3 minutes), or a short screen/video recording if your app supports it. Speak your speech as your chosen character, in first person. Glance at your notes but do not read word-for-word; speak. Use at least two Week 6 vocabulary words. Gestures and pauses are part of the performance.
3
Save your recording and take a screenshot of your memorial space., and in the comments box note your option (A, B, or C) and the two vocabulary words you used. Keep this recording.
