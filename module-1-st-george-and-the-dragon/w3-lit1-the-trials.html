In Chapters 10–11, the danger rises, but the hardest trials Marcellus faces are not only physical. His old certainties are being tested. Watch what is burning away.

🏛️ A LITTLE HISTORY FIRST
When early Christians were threatened, they often did not fight back with weapons. They responded with prayer, with community, and with a stubborn refusal to deny their faith. To a Roman officer trained to meet every threat with force, this response would have looked strange, even weak. Part of Marcellus's trial is learning to see it differently.
📚 THE LESSON

Once a story's main character has heard a new call, the writer puts him through a stretch of tests, one after another, each harder than the last. In Chapters 10–11, the dragon attacks Marcellus's father's estate by night, setting fires, devouring sheep, and leaving the courtyard filled with stench and ruin. Worst of all, Marcellus's father is struck down. His grief boils into vengeance, and he rides out to fight the dragon, but his recklessness nearly gets him killed. Pay attention to a quieter test running underneath the violence: every time the dragon spares him or speaks soothingly, Marcellus's old certainties crack a little more. He was sure strength meant force. A good reader tracks both fires: the one outside, attacking the estate, and the one inside, testing what Marcellus believes. As you read, find one sentence where his old way of seeing the world is challenged.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · WORD WORK
Find a sentence in Chapters 10–11 that uses vengeance, recklessness, or stench. Copy it, then rewrite it one of three ways: swap a near-synonym, stretch it with more detail, or keep its shape but write about your own world. Add a line: what changed, and why.

⚡ CHECK YOURSELF · WHICH KIND OF TRIAL?
For each event, choose whether it is mainly a physical trial or a trial of belief.
1. The dragon sets fire to the estate's buildings and tears apart its sheep in the courtyard.
2. After their fight, the dragon spares Marcellus's life and tells him it has shown him mercy.
3. The creature people call a god behaves like a hungry animal.
