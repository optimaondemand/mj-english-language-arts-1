🏭 COMPOSITIONAL CRAFT
The martyr's ending: the hagiographic tradition (the writing of saints' lives from the early church through the Legenda Aurea) always ends the same way: the death that looks like a defeat but is actually a victory. Panel 6 closes an arc that began with an ordered Roman world and ends with a death that outlasts Rome. Hold the tension: not triumph, not defeat, but testimony, an ending that is also a beginning.
Refusing to resolve: the classical martyr's narrative asks the reader to hold defeat and victory at once. Build a panel that refuses to resolve the tension into one or the other. A future viewer should be able to understand what George's story is about from your six panels alone, even without a word of Lotti's prose.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What does George bring back, and to whom?

📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank space in your VR app to build in. This is your workspace. If you can, review the whole storyboard one last time. The final panel should feel like an ending, not just another panel.
2
Choose your moment from Chapters 22–24 (the martyrdom, the refusal to recant, or how George is remembered) and arrange 3–5 objects. His "return" is not a homecoming but a passage into legacy; let your composition suggest something beyond defeat: endurance, witness, the thing death cannot reach.
3
Add two signed labels using a writable surface or note tool (or jot them in your M365 Reading Journal and keep a card in frame): (a) a title naming the scene from the novel and the chapter; (b) a stage label reading "Story Stage: The Return," plus one sentence answering the focus question.
4
Take two screenshots: a close one of Panel 6 with both labels visible, and a wider panoramic shot showing as much of your full six-panel storyboard as possible. Submit both to the Canvas assignment VR 6.B: Storyboard Week 6, and keep the panoramic for your module project.
