Lesson 1.17
After the Change: A New Way of Seeing
Literature · Week 4
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
When a person changes, the words around him change too.
Chapter 19 shows George acting as his new self for the first time. Today you read closely to notice how the writer's language shifts (the verbs, the images, the feeling) once the change is complete.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to notice how the writer's word choices change after the baptism.
2
I will be able to explain how George's new self acts differently from the old Marcellus.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
St. George and the Dragon, Chapter 19. (Read from your own copy of the book.)
▶
Your Reading Journal (the Word doc in your OneDrive ELA6 folder)
⚡ WARM-UP · CHECK IN FIRST
After a big change in your life, did the world look or feel different afterward? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

A skilled writer doesn't just tell you a character has changed. The writing itself changes to prove it. In Chapter 19, at the courtyard fountain, Marcellus makes a confession of his sins, kneels in the water, and is given the new name George; Agathon then anoints him with oil. Watch how the language around him shifts. The harsh, commanding verbs that once described Marcellus give way to weeping, kneeling, and the "kiss of peace" from former enemies. The man who once ranked everyone by power now lets slaves greet him as "Master" while he calls them brothers. His identity has shifted, and so has the world's texture around him. Even his new name is a quiet argument: George means "farmer," a humble worker, not a soldier. This is the writer's proof that the conversion was real and not just announced. Pick one detail that feels different from the old Marcellus, and you've caught the change in the act.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · WORD WORK
Find a sentence in Chapter 19 that uses baptism, confession, or anoint. Copy it, then rewrite it one of three ways: swap a near-synonym, stretch it with more detail, or keep its shape but write about your own world. Add a line: what changed, and why.
📖 NOW READ
Read Chapter 19. Find one detail (a verb, an image, an action) that feels different now that Marcellus is George. Note it in your journal.

⚡ THINK FIRST, THEN REVEAL
Answer each in your head (or your journal) before you open it. Then check your thinking.
1. After the baptism, slaves and former enemies give George a "kiss of peace," and he weeps. What is that doing?
It shows the change is real. The proud soldier who once ranked everyone by power now receives love from people he once captured, and is moved to tears. The writer proves the conversion through how George now relates to others.
2. The old Marcellus "strode" and "commanded." If George's verbs feel steadier or calmer, why might the writer do that?
Because his source of strength has changed. He no longer acts only from force and rank; the steadier verbs show a courage rooted in conviction rather than command.
3. How does the changed language prove the conversion was real?
A writer could simply announce "George had changed." Instead, the sentences themselves change (the verbs, images, and mood) so the reader feels the transformation rather than just being told about it.
