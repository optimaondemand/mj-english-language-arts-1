Lesson 1.04
Nouns and the Adjectives That Earn Their Place
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A good adjective does work. A weak one just takes up space.
You'll review what nouns and adjectives are, then practice the harder skill: choosing the exact adjective instead of a vague one.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to tell a noun from an adjective and name how they work together.
2
I will be able to replace a vague adjective with a precise one that earns its place.
⚡ WARM-UP
Quick gut check: which sentence sounds stronger to you? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea: soldier, estate, courage. An adjective describes a noun. It answers which one? what kind? how many? The skill that separates plain writing from strong writing is not using more adjectives. It is choosing the right one. "A big horse" tells you almost nothing, because "big" fits a house, a meal, a mistake. But the writer calls Marcellus's horse a "young and strong" horse with an "all-white hide," and you see it at once. When you read this week, watch the adjectives the writer attaches to Marcellus and to the Roman world. Each precise one is a small argument about what that thing really is. Then do the same in your own sentences: keep the noun, and hunt for the one adjective that earns its place.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL · A WORD TO KEEP
precise adjective (tap to see what it means)
A precise adjective is one that fits its noun and almost nothing else. Test it: if the adjective could describe ten unrelated things ("nice," "big," "good"), it isn't pulling its weight yet.
📚 CAPITAL LETTERS DO MORE THAN START NAMES

You already capitalize the names of specific people and places. The same rule reaches further than most students expect. A title shortened into an abbreviation keeps its capital letter: Dr. for Doctor, Mr. for Mister. An initial standing in for part of someone's name is capitalized, with a period after it: J. R. R. Tolkien. An acronym, a word built from the first letter of each word in an organization's name, is capitalized in every letter: NASA, FBI. And the full name of a specific organization is capitalized all the way through, the same as any proper noun: the Red Cross, not "the group." The test is always the one you already use for names: is this one particular, specific thing, or just any old example of its kind?

💪 BEFORE YOU GO

From now on, treat every adjective like it has to audition. If it could describe anything, cut it or replace it. Your writing gets sharper one word at a time.
