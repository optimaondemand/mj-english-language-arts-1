Lesson 1.13
Where, When, How: Prepositional Phrases
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 3
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A few small words can put your reader right in the middle of the action.
You'll learn what a prepositional phrase is and how writers layer them in battle scenes to tell you where, when, and how. A short quiz checks what you've got.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to identify a prepositional phrase and name what it answers.
2
I will be able to add a prepositional phrase to place a reader in a scene.
⚡ WARM-UP
Which sentence lets you "see" more? (No wrong answer. Just notice the difference.)
📚 THE LESSON

A prepositional phrase starts with a small connecting word (in, on, at, from, through, toward, beneath, without, despite) and ends with a noun. That noun is the object of the preposition: into the courtyard, through the smoke, despite the danger. The phrase's job is to add information, and each one answers a question: where (into the courtyard), when (at nightfall), how (with quiet courage), or why / despite what (despite the heat). In action and battle scenes, writers stack these phrases to put you physically inside the moment. You feel where the characters stand, from which direction the dragon comes, and what they endure. As you read, notice how often the writer locates you in space this way. Then, in your own writing, reach for a prepositional phrase whenever a sentence feels like it is floating. It will land your reader somewhere real.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL · THE QUICK TEST
How do I find a prepositional phrase fast? (tap)
Find the little connecting word (in, on, through, toward, despite...), then read to the noun that finishes it. That whole chunk is the phrase. Ask: does it tell me where, when, how, or why? If yes, you found it.
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Reopen Chapters 10–14 and find a single action paragraph. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Underline three prepositional phrases in your notes and label what each one answers.
⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE PHRASE ANSWER?
For each bold prepositional phrase, choose what it tells the reader. Pick one for instant feedback.
1. The dragon leaped into the courtyard.
2. The Christians prayed with quiet courage.
3. The dragon attacked at nightfall.
