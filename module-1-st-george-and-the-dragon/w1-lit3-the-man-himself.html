Lesson 1.03
The Man Himself: Reading a Character's Actions
Literature · Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What a person does under pressure tells you who they are.
You have seen Marcellus's world. Today, in Chapter 4, you watch the man inside it: how he treats people, what he chooses, what he will not do. The craft term for how a writer reveals a character is characterization, and one of its strongest forms is showing you what a person does. A character is built out of actions, and you are about to read his.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to infer a character trait from what a character does, not just what he says.
2
I will be able to rate how strongly an action in Chapter 4 reveals a trait, and explain my rating.

▶
St. George and the Dragon, Chapter 4. (Read from your own copy of the book.)
▶

⚡ WARM-UP · CHECK IN FIRST
If you wanted to know what someone is really like, which would tell you more? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

When a writer wants you to know a character, one of the strongest tools is action: what the person does, especially when a choice is hard. This is characterization through action, and it is more convincing than any label the writer could attach. If the writer simply told you "Marcellus had self-control," you might or might not believe it. But in Chapter 4 you watch him sit through Lucius's loud, drunken feast, hearing guests insult soldiers and cheer the persecution of Christians, and quietly choose not to react so he will not insult his host. You draw the conclusion yourself, and a conclusion you draw is one you trust. Watch the people around him too. The whipped slave's body hung by the road tells you what kind of master Lucius is, and Regina's calm, sober command in the middle of the chaos tells you she is someone to reckon with. A reader who can point to the action behind a trait is doing real interpretation, not guessing. The writer is also setting something up. The steadiness Marcellus shows here is the very quality that will later pull him toward a truth his world rejects. Today you meet the man whose character will be tested by everything that comes next.

📖 NOW READ
Read Chapter 4. Mark two actions Marcellus takes that tell you something about his character. Note them in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal.

⚡ CHECK YOURSELF · HOW MUCH DOES THIS REVEAL?
For each action, rate how strongly it reveals a real trait in Marcellus: a little, somewhat, or a lot. There is a best answer for each.
1. As guests grow drunk and insulting, Marcellus pretends to hear none of it so he will not embarrass Lucius.
2. When Lucius presents him to the room, Marcellus stands squarely and holds his head high.
3. Marcellus feels uncomfortable at Lucius's estate without quite knowing why.
