Legacy: What the Whole Story Leaves Behind
Literature · Week 6
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
An ending is also a beginning.
In the final chapter, George's story does not stop at his death. It becomes a legacy that outlasts the empire that killed him. Today you read the close of the book and step back to see the whole arc, from Marcellus the Roman officer to George the witness. This lesson sets up your culminating essay.

1
I will be able to trace the change from Marcellus to George across the whole novel.
2
I will be able to judge how strongly a statement about the book's meaning is supported by the whole story.

St. George and the Dragon, Chapter 24 (and your notes on the whole novel). (Read from your own copy of the book.)
▶

📚 THE LESSON

A strong ending does two things at once: it closes the story and opens its meaning outward. The final chapter spends only a few sentences on George's death: he and his companions are captured and executed during the persecution. But then it leaps ahead: after Galerius dies and the persecution ends, the Christian communities George protected thrive, and they remember him as a martyr, "the courageous and faithful leader who slew a dragon and protected Christians." His memory, not the empire that killed him, is what lasts. Now look back across the whole novel. The book began with Marcellus giving his loyalty to power simply because it was powerful. It ends with George giving his life rather than deny what he believes is true. That is the arc, from false loyalty to faithful witness, and every chapter has been a step along it. When you read for a book's meaning, you do not seize on one line; you test your idea against the whole story. A reading that the beginning, middle, and end all support is a strong reading. A reading that only one scene supports is a weak one. Today you practice exactly that judgment, because your culminating essay will ask you to make a claim about this book and prove it from across all six weeks.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · WORD WORK
Find a sentence in Chapter 24 that uses martyr, courageous, or faithful. Copy it, then rewrite it one of three ways: swap a near-synonym, stretch it with more detail, or keep its shape but write about someone whose memory lasts. Add one line: what changed, and why.

WHERE HE ENDED
George · witness · loyalty to what he believes is true · faithful at any cost
