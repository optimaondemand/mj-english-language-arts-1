📖 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
St. George and the Dragon
Michael Lotti, Chapters 15–19
Lotti's conversion scenes are built on contrast: what Marcellus was, and what he is becoming. Water means death and rebirth at once; light shifts before and after the baptism; the name itself is exchanged like a garment. You will build two groupings (what Marcellus carries before the threshold, and what he carries after), then record a short reflection as George, standing between them.
🏭 LITERARY & HISTORICAL FRAME
Baptism as bodily event: baptism by full immersion was the standard rite of the early church, drawing on Jewish purification traditions and John the Baptist's practice in the Jordan. The descent-and-return pattern is not a metaphor for early Christians; it happens to the body. The person goes under and comes up. In the language of the early church the water is at once tomb and womb: the old self buried, the new self born.
Building a scene to understand it: if you can render a written image in another medium, you have understood it. Your before-and-after arrangement is an argument about what the threshold costs and gives. If you can build it convincingly in space, you have understood what the writer wrote.
The vice this session names: what Marcellus carries before includes false allegiance: loyalty given to power because it is power, not because it is worthy. What he carries after is honest self-knowledge. The distance between the two groupings is the distance between those two things.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION (speak as George)
What did I leave behind when I crossed this threshold, and what did I find waiting on the other side?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset
Activity type: solo; you build and record on your own, at your own pace
Tools you'll use: a blank space, simple objects you can arrange into two groups with a marked line or gap between them, and a writable surface or note tool for your two labels. [Teacher: confirm the build/objects/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
To start: open a blank space. You are not touring a ready-made world; you build the BEFORE and AFTER groups yourself and mark your own dividing line between them.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank space and mark a dividing line. This is your workspace. Set down a line, a gap, or a single object to mark a threshold across the middle. It separates your two groupings: BEFORE on one side, AFTER on the other.
2
Build Grouping A: BEFORE. Place 2–3 objects representing who Marcellus is before his conversion: his rank, his allegiance, what he worshipped, the weight of what he is leaving behind. Add a signed label saying what the grouping represents (one sentence) and one Week 4 vocabulary word that fits.
3
Build Grouping B: AFTER. Place 2–3 objects representing who George is becoming after his baptism: a new allegiance, a new name, rebirth, sacred light. The two groupings should look different from each other. Add a signed label saying what it represents (one sentence) and a different Week 4 vocabulary word.
4
Stand at the threshold and record. Make a voice recording (2–3 minutes), or a short screen/video recording if your app supports it, speaking as George just after his baptism. Answer the focus question above. Speak as George, not about him; use at least two Week 4 vocabulary words; gesture toward the two groupings as you speak.
5
Take a screenshot showing both groupings and the threshold between them. Submit your recording and the screenshot to the Canvas assignment VR 4.A: Before and After.
