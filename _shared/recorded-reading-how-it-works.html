One take, four segments, no stopping in between. You say where the passage comes from, you read it, you respond to it, and you sign off.
Each module is produced as a different kind of programme: an audio drama, a poetry broadcast, a gallery audio guide. Your assignment page names the format for your module and gives its run sheet, including what each segment is called. Segment 02 is the reading, in every format, every time.
You are not being graded on having a good voice, and this is not a performance. You are being graded on choosing a real passage, reading it so a listener can follow, and saying something true about it.

📝 PART 1 · SAY WHERE IT COMES FROM
Before you read a single line of the passage, tell your listener where they are in the book and what is about to happen. Your assignment page gives the exact opening for your module's format, and other wording is permitted if it carries the same facts.
Some books have no chapters. When that happens, name the part of the story instead and say roughly where it falls: near the beginning, just after the storm, at the very end.
📖 PART 2 · READ IT ALOUD
Read the passage as written, from the copy you are using. Between 300 and 500 words, which is about one to two pages in most editions. Check the word count so you are not guessing.
Read it out loud twice before you record.
1
The first read is for finding the words you stumble on. Look up the ones you do not know how to say.
2
The second read is for deciding where the sentences breathe. If you run out of air in the middle of a sentence, that is the sentence telling you where the pause goes.
Nobody sounds good on a cold first read. Almost the entire difference between a rushed recording and a clear one is those two practice reads.
These books are full of names that are hard on sight — Pasikrates, Beaumains, Percivale, Diocletian. Say the ones in your passage out loud a few times before you record. A reasonable attempt at an unfamiliar name is full credit; skipping it is not.
👂 WHAT A LISTENER NOTICES
HARD TO FOLLOW
Every sentence at the same speed
No pause at the full stops
Voice drops away at the end of lines
Skips a word and keeps going
Reads a question like a statement
EASY TO FOLLOW
Slows down where the meaning is dense
Stops at the full stops
Stays audible to the last word
Goes back and re-reads the line
Lets a question sound like a question
Nothing in the right-hand column is a talent. Each one is a decision you can make before you press record.
💭 PART 3 · THEN TALK ABOUT IT
Do not stop the recording when the passage ends. Keep going and answer at least two of the questions below. You do not have to say which question you are answering.
1
What does this passage show about the character that you did not know before it?
2
Why did you choose this passage instead of another one?
3
Name one word the author chose here that a different word could not have done. What does it do?
4
What question does this passage leave you with?
5
How does this passage change your answer to your module’s central question?
6
What did you notice reading it out loud that you had not noticed reading it silently?
Your module may word one or two of these slightly differently. The assignment page is the version that counts.
That last one is worth trying at least once. Reading aloud slows you to the speed of the sentences, and people almost always hear something they had skimmed past.
📹 HOW TO RECORD AND SUBMIT
1
Open the assignment and click Record / Upload Media.
2
Choose audio. Video is allowed if you prefer it, but it is never required and it is never worth extra points.
3
Record all four segments in one take. Then listen back to the first ten seconds before you submit. That is the check that catches a muted microphone, and it takes ten seconds.
4
Submit. You get two attempts, so if you are unhappy with a take you may record again.
If the Canvas recorder will not work on your device, record with any voice recorder or phone and upload the audio file to the same assignment instead. Either way is full credit. [Teacher: confirm the media recorder is enabled and that microphones are reachable on school devices.]
