Lesson 3.02
Excalibur: What Makes a Leader Worthy
Literature · Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A writer shows you who a leader is by what he does, not by saying it.
Today Arthur receives a second sword, Excalibur, from the Lady of the Lake. Watch the choices he makes around it: the promise he gives, the price Merlin points out. The craft term for how a writer builds a person in your mind through actions, words, and choices is characterization.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain how Green uses characterization to show what kind of king Arthur is.
2
I will be able to explain why Merlin says the scabbard is worth more than the blade.

📚 THE LESSON

Characterization is how a writer builds a person in your mind. Sometimes a writer tells you straight out, but more often a writer shows you through what a character does, says, and chooses. After Arthur breaks his first sword, Merlin leads him to a magic lake, where an arm clothed in white samite lifts a sword called Excalibur from the water. The Lady of the Lake offers it in exchange for a future gift Arthur swears to grant. Watch what that exchange reveals: Arthur keeps his word, trusts the bargain, and takes both the blade and its jewelled scabbard. Then notice Merlin's strange remark that the scabbard matters more than the sword, because it guards its wearer from wounds. The point is not the magic. The point is that a good king is valued for protection and steadiness, not just for striking power. Your job today is to read Arthur's actions as characterization and ask what they argue about the kind of leader he is becoming.
