Lesson 3.11
The Words of Honor
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 2
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Learn the words of a world, and the world opens up to you.
This week's words come from Gawain's story: the gear and signs of a knight, and the qualities his honor is tested by. You'll meet them where they live in the chapter. Reading a word inside a real sentence lets you use the surrounding words to figure out its meaning. The craft term for those surrounding hints is context clues. Then you'll make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's words of honor correctly in the context of Gawain's story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
This week's words name a knight's honor. Which idea feels most important to you in a friendship or a team? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below belongs to "Sir Gawain and the Green Knight." Some name the world of the tale (the Yule feast, the pentangle on his shield, the green girdle). Others name what Gawain's honor is measured by: his integrity, the covenant he keeps, the peril he faces, and the valour he shows.

WordWhat it means here
integritybeing honest and whole in your conduct, doing right even when no one is watching
covenanta solemn, binding agreement or promise (the exchange bargain Gawain makes)
perilserious danger or risk (the deadly blow Gawain must face)
valourgreat courage in danger, as when Gawain takes the Green Knight's challenge
courtesypolite, respectful, honorable behavior, a knight's expected manner
pentanglea five-pointed star; Gawain's shield bears it as the "Star of Logres," a sign of his virtues
girdlea belt or sash worn at the waist; here, the magic green lace Gawain hides
Yulethe old word for the Christmas season, when the feast and the challenge take place
concealto hide or keep secret (Gawain conceals the girdle from his host)
temptationa strong pull to do something you know you should not
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Week-1 Reading Journal lesson):
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside "Sir Gawain and the Green Knight". (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.
