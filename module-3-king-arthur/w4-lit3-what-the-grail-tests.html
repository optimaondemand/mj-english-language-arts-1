What the Quest Was Really Testing

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The quest gave the kingdom its highest hour, and then began to empty it.
Galahad achieves the Grail, and the Quest ends. Today you name the theme of the whole Grail story in your own words, and you notice something the story does quietly: by sending the knights away, the Quest begins to scatter the Round Table. One event can be both a triumph and the start of a loss.

1
I will be able to state the Grail Quest's theme in one sentence about purity and worth.
2
I will be able to explain how the Quest is both Logres's highest hour and the start of its decline.

📚 THE LESSON

Now you can name the Grail Quest's theme, the idea about life it argues: only the most worthy can find the Grail, and worthiness is measured by purity of heart, not by strength or fame. Galahad, the purest, achieves it. Arthur calls this "the highest hour of this our holy realm of Logres." But watch what the same quest does to the kingdom. To seek the Grail, the knights must scatter across the land, and many never return. The fellowship that filled the Round Table is thinned. The very quest that proves Logres at its best also begins to empty it. That is a quiet, hard truth the story trusts you to see: a triumph and a loss can be the same event. As you read the end, hold both halves together: the glory of the achievement, and the cost of the scattering.

⚡ COMMIT FIRST, THEN COMPARE
Before you read the end, write your own one-sentence answer: what does the Grail actually test in a knight?
Reveal one strong way to say it
A clear version: "The Grail tests purity of heart, not strength or fame, so the worthiest knight finds it while the strongest does not." Compare yours. If your sentence names inner worth over outward greatness, you have the theme. Keep your wording; it can become a sentence in your essay.
