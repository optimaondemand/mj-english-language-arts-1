📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A chair no one may sit in. A cup no one may see clearly. The story is telling you something.
An empty seat at the Round Table, the Siege Perilous, has waited for one destined knight. Sir Galahad takes it and lives. Then the Holy Grail enters the hall in a blaze of light. Today you learn to read a symbol: an object that stands for an idea larger than itself.

📚 THE LESSON

A symbol is an object that stands for an idea larger than itself. This chapter is built from symbols. The Siege Perilous is an empty seat that kills anyone unworthy who sits in it; when Sir Galahad sits and lives, the seat has named him the chosen knight. Then, at the Pentecost feast, the hermit leads Galahad in, and the Holy Grail enters covered in white cloth, so full of light "that none might behold it." Notice that the Grail is something you cannot look at directly. That is the point: it stands for a goodness too pure for ordinary eyes. A story uses symbols to make an inner idea (worthiness, purity, the highest goal) into something you can see on the page. As you read, do not just track what happens. Ask what each strange object means.

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL · A TERM TO KEEP
symbol (tap to see what it means)
A symbol is a concrete thing (an object, place, or image) that stands for an idea bigger than itself. The Grail is a cup, but it stands for the highest spiritual goal; the Siege Perilous is a chair, but it stands for being chosen and worthy. Test it: if an object keeps drawing attention and seems to mean more than it is, it is probably a symbol.
