Lesson 3.10
Strong Verbs and Steady Tense
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 2
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The verb is the engine of the sentence. Keep it strong, and keep it steady.
You'll review what a verb is and what verb tense does, then practice two skills: choosing a strong, exact verb, and keeping your tense consistent so the reader is never confused about when something happens.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to identify a verb and name its tense (past, present, or future).
2
I will be able to fix a sentence that switches tense by mistake, and swap a weak verb for a strong one.
⚡ WARM-UP
Which verb makes the strongest picture of the Green Knight entering the hall? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

A verb shows action or being: ride, strike, wait, is, became. Verb tense tells the reader when the action happens: past (rode), present (rides), or future (will ride). Two habits make verbs work for you. First, choose a strong, exact verb. "Gawain struck the blow" shows more than "Gawain did the blow," because a precise verb paints the action. Second, keep your tense steady. If you start a story in the past, stay in the past. A sentence like "Gawain rode to the chapel and waits for the Green Knight" jolts the reader, because it slips from past to present for no reason. The fix is to match: "Gawain rode to the chapel and waited." When you write about Gawain this week, pick verbs that show the action and keep them all in the same tense.

PastGawain struck the blow.already happened
PresentGawain strikes the blow.happening now

📖 FROM THE BOOK
Reopen "Sir Gawain and the Green Knight" and find two strong action verbs Green uses (for example, a verb for how the Green Knight rides, or how Gawain swings the axe). (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each, and write the tense of each.
⚡ TRY IT · STRONG AND STEADY
Answer each row about verbs and tense. Pick one and you'll get instant feedback.
1. Which word is the verb? "Gawain quickly beheaded the green giant."
2. What tense is the verb in "Gawain will return in a year and a day"?
3. Which sentence keeps a steady tense?
💪 BEFORE YOU GO

From now on, when you revise, circle your verbs. Ask two questions: is this the strongest verb I can use, and are all my verbs in the same tense? Strong, steady verbs make writing move.
