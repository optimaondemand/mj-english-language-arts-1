📚 STOP 4 · THE WORLD OF THE BOOK · READ THE FEATURES

You can find a source and you can skim it. Now learn to read the parts of a page that are not sentences. On an informational page, the photograph is not decoration. The caption underneath tells you what you are supposed to notice in it, and it often carries a fact that appears nowhere in the paragraphs. A map answers "where" in two seconds, faster than any paragraph can. A diagram shows how the parts of a thing fit together, which is why armor and castles are almost always explained with one. A heading is a promise about what the next section will do, and a bold term marks a word the writer expects you not to know yet. Good readers of nonfiction look at all of that before reading the paragraphs, because it builds a frame to hang the sentences on. Try it deliberately this week. Open your page, and before you read a single paragraph, study every picture, caption, map, and bold word. You will find you already know a surprising amount.
