A Name Earned, Not Given

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
He was noble the whole time. He chose to make us watch him earn it.
The kitchen boy is revealed to be Sir Gareth, Sir Gawain's youngest brother and a nephew of King Arthur, noble by birth all along. Today you name the chapter's theme, the idea about life the story argues: that worth is shown by deeds and patience, not by the rank you are born to. You'll also see how the author uses a planned reveal to make that idea land.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to state the chapter's theme in one sentence about worth and rank.
2
I will be able to explain why Gareth chooses to serve in the kitchen instead of claiming his rank.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
The close of “Sir Gareth, or The Knight of the Kitchen” (the reveal of his name and family). (Read from your own copy of the book.)
▶
Your Reading Journal (the Word doc in your OneDrive ELA6 folder)
⚡ WARM-UP · CHECK IN FIRST
If you could already have something just by claiming it, would you still want to earn it? (No wrong answer. Just check in.)
📚 THE LESSON

A theme is the idea about life a story argues through its events. This chapter's theme is plain once the reveal arrives: the kitchen boy is Sir Gareth, a king's nephew, noble from the first page. Green hid that on purpose. By keeping the name secret, he makes us judge Gareth only by his deeds, and that is the whole argument: worth is proven by what you do, not by the rank you are born to. Notice that Gareth's nobility is not the surprise the story cares about. The surprise is that someone who could have demanded a knight's place chose instead to serve a year in the kitchen and earn it. The reveal does not change Gareth. It changes us. We realize the courtesy and patience we admired were never the manners of a servant trying to rise; they were the real character of a knight who refused to lean on his rank. When you read, watch how the reveal makes the earlier kitchen chapters mean more, not less.

⚡ COMMIT FIRST, THEN COMPARE
Before you read the ending, write your own one-sentence guess: what is this chapter trying to say about worth and rank?
Reveal one strong way to say it
A clear version: "True worth is shown by how you act, not by the rank you are born into, so Gareth earns his knighthood by service before he ever claims it by blood." Compare yours. If your sentence names deeds over rank, you've got the theme. Keep your wording in your journal; it can become a sentence in your essay.
📖 NOW READ
Read the close of the chapter, where Beaumains is named Sir Gareth and his family is revealed. As you read, ask: now that I know who he is, do the kitchen chapters look different? Note your answer in your Reading Journal.
