📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A good comparison starts long before you write. It starts with sorting what you already know.
No new chapters this week. Today you gather the evidence for your essay by laying four knights side by side: Gawain, Launcelot, Gareth, and Percivale. The craft work for the week is comparison and contrast: seeing where two knights are alike and, more usefully, where they differ. Each difference is a place your argument can stand.

📚 THE LESSON

Comparison and contrast is the engine of your essay. To compare two knights well, you do not just describe each one in turn. You hold them up to the same question and see how their answers differ. The question this module asks is: what makes a good knight? Green gives each of his four knights a distinct answer. Gawain is the most courteous knight, brave enough to take the Green Knight's deadly game, yet he has one honest failing when he hides the magic girdle out of fear. Launcelot is the greatest knight in arms, generous and gentle, but his divided heart lets him only glimpse the Grail and finally breaks the fellowship. Gareth is noble by birth yet earns his honor by humble service in the kitchen, proving worth comes from deeds, not rank. Percivale is unworldly and innocent, one of the few pure enough to draw near the Grail. Notice that none of them is simply "the best." Each is good in a different way, and each has limits. That is what makes them worth comparing: the differences are real, and the text gives you specific moments to prove them. Today, sort what you know, pick your two, and find the difference that will become your claim.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · WORD WORK
Write one sentence comparing your two knights that uses whereas or unlike (for example, "Whereas Gareth earns honor by service, Launcelot is born to it but loses it"). Then rewrite it once more, swapping in a different comparison word. Add one line: which version makes the contrast sharper, and why?
