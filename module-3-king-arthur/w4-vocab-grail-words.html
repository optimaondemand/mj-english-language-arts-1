The Words of the Quest
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 4
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The Grail story uses a special set of words. Knowing them, you read it more closely.
This week's words come from the Grail Quest: the holy feast, the sacred objects, the language of worthiness. You'll meet them where they live in the story. Reading a word inside a real sentence lets you use the surrounding words to figure out its meaning. The craft term for those surrounding hints is context clues. Then you'll make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's Grail-quest words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
Several of this week's words name sacred or special things. Which way of learning a word helps you most? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below appears in or describes the Grail Quest. Some name the holy day and the sacred objects; others name the qualities the Grail measures in a knight. Watching for them as you read will sharpen what the quest is doing.

WordWhat it means here
Siegehere, a seat (the Siege Perilous, the dangerous chair reserved for the chosen knight)
Pentecosta Christian feast day; in the story, the holy day on which the Grail appears
hermita holy man who lives alone in prayer; one guides Galahad to the Siege Perilous
samitea rich, heavy silk cloth (the Grail is covered in white samite)
purityfreedom from sin or fault; cleanness of heart, the quality the Grail tests
sacredholy; connected to God and worthy of deep respect
visiona sight of something holy or beyond the ordinary world
achieveto succeed in completing a great and difficult goal (to achieve the Grail)
questa long, difficult journey undertaken to reach a goal
worthydeserving respect or reward because of one's good qualities
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Week-1 Reading Journal lesson):
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside the Grail chapters. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.

2. The Grail could be achieved only by a knight of perfect ______ of heart.
3. In the end, only Galahad could ______ the Holy Grail.

💪 BEFORE YOU GO

Words like purity, worthy, and achieve are precise tools for your essay's argument about what makes a knight good. A word you have rewritten three times is a word you can use on purpose. Keep your rewrites in your Reading Journal.
