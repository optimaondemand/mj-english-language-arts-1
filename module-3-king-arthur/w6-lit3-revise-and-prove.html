Lesson 3.33
Revise and Prove: Make the Evidence Honest
Literature · Week 6 · Project Week
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
An argument is only as honest as its evidence.
This is the last literature lesson of the module. Today you make sure your essay's evidence is real, accurate, and fairly used: an early, a middle, and a late moment for each knight, each quoted or paraphrased correctly from your own copy of the book. The craft skill is revision, looking again at your own work to make it true and clear, not just longer.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to check that I have an early, middle, and late moment of evidence for each knight.
2
I will be able to tell the difference between honest evidence and a detail I made up or twisted.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
Your essay draft and your own copy of the book, to confirm and key every quotation. (No new chapters this week.)
▶
Your Reading Journal notes from Weeks 2–5 (the Word doc in your OneDrive ELA6 folder)
⚡ WARM-UP · CHECK IN FIRST
When you revise your own writing, what do you usually do? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

Revision is not the same as fixing typos. It means looking again at your argument and asking whether it is true and whether the proof holds. Your essay needs, for each knight, an early, a middle, and a late moment from the book, so your reader sees the knight across the whole story, not just one scene. As you check each one, hold yourself to a simple test of honesty. The evidence must be real: it happens in Green's book. It must be accurate: the quotation matches the words on the page, and the paraphrase does not change what happened. And it must be fairly used: you do not bend a moment to mean something it does not. For example, it is fair to say Launcelot's love for Guinevere breaks the fellowship, because the book shows that. It is not fair to claim Launcelot meant to kill Gareth, because the book is clear it was an accident. Twisting evidence may make your argument look stronger for a moment, but it makes it dishonest, and a careful reader will catch it. Today, reread every piece of evidence in your draft, confirm it against your own copy of the book, and fix anything that is missing, inaccurate, or unfair.

💡 TRY THIS IN YOUR READING JOURNAL · WORD WORK
For each knight, list your three moments and label them early / middle / late. Beside each, write one phrase: real, accurate, fair? If any moment fails the test, replace it with one that passes. Add one line: which piece of evidence got stronger after you checked it?
📖 NO NEW READING · CONFIRM EVERY QUOTE
No new chapters this week. Open your own copy of the book to the pages you are quoting, and check each quotation word for word. Fix any quote that does not match the page exactly.

⚡ CHECK YOURSELF · HONEST EVIDENCE OR NOT?
For each statement about the book, choose whether it is fair evidence to use. Pick one and you'll get instant feedback.
1. "Gareth proves his humility by serving a year in the kitchen under Sir Kay."
2. "Launcelot deliberately murders Gareth to win the battle."
3. "Gawain's one failing is hiding the magic girdle out of fear for his life."
4. "Percivale is a coward who abandons the Grail Quest out of fear."
