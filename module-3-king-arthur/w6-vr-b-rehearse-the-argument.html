📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
Say It Out Loud Before You Write It
The spoken rehearsal of your comparison essay
Speaking an argument out loud tests whether it holds together. You will record a short spoken version of your essay: state your claim about what makes a good knight, walk through your two knights with one moment each, fairly name the other side and answer it, and close with what the comparison reveals. Then you write the full essay in Word, using what you heard yourself say.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Said out loud, does your argument actually prove that one knight better fits what makes a good knight?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset, or any voice recorder if VR is unavailable
Activity type: solo. No live session, no scheduled time, no classmates present.
Tool you'll use: a voice recorder. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your essay plan from VR 3.6A, with your claim, both knights' evidence, the fair other side, and what the comparison reveals.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open your VR app on your own. Out loud, rehearse once from your plan: state your claim, then walk through your two knights.
2
Make a voice recording (2–3 minutes). A short screen or video recording also works. In it: (a) state your claim about what makes a good knight; (b) give Knight 1 with one moment from the book; (c) give Knight 2 with one moment; (d) fairly name the other side and answer it; (e) close with what the comparison reveals.
3
Listen back once. Note the one spot that sounded weakest out loud. That is the part of your essay to strengthen when you write the final draft.
4
Save your recording. with a one-sentence note on what you found hardest to say out loud. Then write your full comparison essay in Word for the module project.
