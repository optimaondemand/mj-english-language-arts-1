📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The story hides who a person really is, then makes you watch them prove it.
A tall young man comes to Arthur's court and asks for nothing but food for a year. Sir Kay scorns him, puts him to work in the kitchen, and nicknames him Beaumains, which means "Fair Hands." Today you watch how Green keeps the young man's true name hidden and lets you judge him by what he does. The craft idea for this is characterization: the ways an author shows you who a person is.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what characterization is and how Green uses it to hide and then reveal who Beaumains is.
2
I will be able to point to one action that shows what kind of man the kitchen boy is, even before we learn his name.

📚 THE LESSON

When a story hides a character's name and rank, it forces you to judge that character only by what they do. That is characterization at work. At a Pentecost feast a tall, fair young man asks Arthur for one thing: meat and drink for a year, not a horse or armour. The steward Sir Kay decides the youth must be a poor peasant, sets him to work in the kitchen, and mockingly calls him Beaumains, "Fair Hands." For a year the young man carries water, turns the spit, and takes Kay's jeers "always gentle and patient," never snapping back. Green never tells you the youth is noble. He shows you, through small actions, a person whose calm does not depend on what others think of him. Notice the verbs Green gives him. He serves, he endures, he waits. A character's verbs are quiet arguments about who they are. Your job today is to catch those arguments before the story spells anything out.
