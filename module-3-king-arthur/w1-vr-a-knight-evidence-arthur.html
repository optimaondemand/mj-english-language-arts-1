📝 WHAT YOU ARE DOING IN VR THIS WEEK
King Arthur and His Knights of the Round Table
Roger Lancelyn Green · "The Two Swords" · read from your own copy
Think of VR here as a giant whiteboard you can walk around in. It is a tool for thinking, the way a word processor is a tool for writing. Today you start a workspace you will keep all module long: your Knight Evidence board. You will gather what "The Two Swords" actually shows about Arthur and about what makes someone fit to be king. Everything you collect feeds your module project, the comparison essay "What Makes a Good Knight?"
You are not visiting a ready-made world. You are building a board of your own evidence, using details you found in the reading.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What gives Arthur the right to rule, and what does the story say a good leader should be?
💡 BEFORE YOU PUT ON YOUR HEADSET
Device: your VR headset (solo, at your own pace)
Tool you'll use: a blank board or open space where you can place notes and simple objects. [Teacher: confirm the build/notes/recording tools students use in the current VR platform]
Bring with you: your own copy of the book and your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Reading Journal lesson), open to your Week 1 notes.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Open a blank board (or clear space) in your VR app. Title it Knight Evidence. This is your workspace for the whole module.
2
Make a note for the question the chapter is testing: who deserves to be king? Write it in one sentence so you can answer it with evidence below.
3
Make four to six evidence notes, one detail each, from "The Two Swords": the sword set in the stone and anvil, lettered with who would be "the true-born king"; the grown knights who pull and fail; young Arthur drawing the sword at a touch when he rides back for Kay's blade; Sir Ector kneeling to him once he draws it again; later, the Lady of the Lake giving Arthur the second sword, Excalibur, in exchange for a promise; Merlin saying the scabbard that guards its wearer is worth more than the blade. Use the details you actually noticed.
4
Add one or two trait notes that name the quality each detail shows. For example, drawing the sword without knowing what it was shows honesty and humility, not bragging. These traits become your first standard for what a good knight or leader should be.
5
Take a screenshot of your finished board. You'll submit it in the Canvas assignment VR 3.1A: Brainstorm Board. (No headset? Build the same board on paper or in a slide and submit a photo.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
My board names the question the chapter tests and has four to six real details from "The Two Swords."
☐
I named one or two traits each detail shows, as my first standard for a good knight.
☐
I submitted a screenshot of my board to VR 3.1A.
