The Words That Name a Good Knight
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 6
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The exact word for a virtue makes your argument sound exact.
Your essay argues what makes a good knight, so it needs precise words for the virtues you are weighing. This week gathers the chivalry words that ran through the whole book and that you'll reach for in your final draft. You'll meet each one, then make one your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use the chivalry words precisely to describe a knight's virtues and flaws.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
Which virtue do you think Green most values in a knight? (No wrong answer. This may become your claim.)
📚 THE WORDS YOUR ESSAY NEEDS

Every word below ran through the whole book and names a virtue, a flaw, or a move you'll make in your essay. These are the precise words that will make your comparison sound exact instead of vague. Many come from Latin or French, and the root often unlocks the meaning.

WordWhat it means here
chivalrythe code of honor, courage, and courtesy that knights were expected to live by (the "model of chivalry" of Logres)
courtesypolite, respectful, considerate behavior, especially Gawain's; more than manners, it is honoring others
humilitya modest view of your own importance; Gareth's choice to serve rather than claim rank (from Latin humilis, "low")
devotiondeep, loyal commitment to a person or purpose, like Percivale's single-minded search for the Grail
loyaltyfirm, faithful support for someone; the bond that holds the fellowship together until it splits
flawa fault or weakness in an otherwise admirable person, like Launcelot's divided heart
noblehaving high birth or, more importantly, high character: generous, honorable, and brave
contrasta clear difference between two things set side by side; the move at the heart of your essay
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above, ideally one you will use in your essay. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal:
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about a different knight.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM YOUR ESSAY
Reopen your essay draft. Find two places where you wrote a vague word like "good" or "nice," and replace each with one of this week's precise words. (Check your own copy of the book if you want to confirm a knight's virtue.) Note both swaps in your Reading Journal.
⚡ TRY IT · THE RIGHT WORD
Choose the word that fits each sentence about the knights.
1. By hiding his royal birth and serving in the kitchen, Gareth shows great ______.
2. Launcelot is the greatest knight, but his love for the queen is his fatal ______.
3. To set Gawain and Gareth side by side and show their difference is to draw a ______.

💪 BEFORE YOU GO

When you write your final draft, name the virtue exactly: not "good" but courteous, humble, or loyal. A precise word is the difference between an essay that gestures and one that argues.
