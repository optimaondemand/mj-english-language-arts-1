The Words of a Kingdom's Fall
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 5
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The right word names exactly what went wrong.
This week's words come from the end of the legend: betrayal, a deadly wound, the sword's return, the dark barge to Avalon. You'll meet each word where it lives in the chapters, then make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways. A word's root often unlocks its meaning, so watch for the Latin hiding inside.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
Which of this week's ideas feels heaviest to you? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below appears in this week's chapters, where Green brings the legend to its end. Some name the betrayal that breaks the kingdom; others name the wound, the sword's strange return, and the dark voyage to Avalon. Read each word inside its sentence in the book before you use it.

WordWhat it means here
treasonthe crime of betraying your king or country (from Latin tradere, "to hand over")
traitora person who betrays someone who trusts them, like Mordred betraying Arthur
mortallyin a way that causes death; a mortal wound is one a person cannot survive (from Latin mors, "death")
brandishedwaved or swung a weapon in the air, often to show power (the hand brandishes Excalibur three times)
bargea long, flat boat moved across calm water (the black-veiled barge that carries Arthur to Avalon)
fellowshipa company of people bound together by loyalty and a shared purpose, like the knights of the Round Table
kindreda person's family or relatives (Gawain mourns his slain kindred)
vengeanceharm done to someone in return for a wrong they caused; payback for an injury
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways:
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside the Book Four chapters and the Epilogue. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.
⚡ TRY IT · THE RIGHT WORD
Choose the word that fits each sentence from the end of the legend.
1. By seizing the throne while Arthur was away, Mordred was guilty of ______.
2. Arthur kills Mordred, but he is himself ______ wounded and cannot survive.
3. The hand rose from the water and ______ Excalibur three times before it vanished.
💪 BEFORE YOU GO

A word you have rewritten three times is a word you can use on purpose. Precise words like treason and mortally will make your essay sound exact instead of vague.
