Lesson 3.05
The Words of Kingship
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Learn the words of a world, and the world opens up to you.
This week's words come from "The Two Swords": the gear of a knight, and the qualities a king is measured by. You'll meet them where they live in the chapter. Reading a word inside a real sentence lets you use the surrounding words to figure out its meaning. The craft term for those surrounding hints is context clues. Then you'll make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's kingship words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
Some of this week's words name things; others name qualities. Which kind of word do you find harder to pin down? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below belongs to "The Two Swords." Some name a knight's world (the anvil, the scabbard, the silk called samite, the contest called a joust). Others name what a true king is measured by: his right to rule, his fate, and his bravery. Watch for them in the chapter.

WordWhat it means here
sovereigna king or supreme ruler; having the highest power (Arthur becomes Britain's sovereign)
destinythe future that seems set for someone; what a person is meant to become (Arthur's destiny as king)
valorgreat courage and bravery, especially in danger or battle
chivalrythe medieval code of honor, courage, and courtesy that knights were expected to live by
anvila heavy iron block a blacksmith hammers metal against; here, the sword is fixed in it
scabbardthe sheath or case that holds and protects a sword's blade
samitea rich, heavy silk fabric used in the Middle Ages (the arm "clothed in white samite")
jousta contest in which two armored knights on horseback charge each other with lances
enchantera person who uses magic; a wizard (Merlin is the enchanter who guides Arthur)
realma kingdom; the land and people a ruler governs (the Realm of Logres)
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Week-1 Reading Journal lesson):
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside "The Two Swords". (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.
⚡ TRY IT · THE RIGHT WORD
Choose the word that fits each sentence from the world of the legend.
1. Once Arthur draws the sword, he is named the true ______ of Britain.
2. The knights of Logres were praised for their ______ when they rode against cruel enemies.
3. Merlin hid the infant because he knew the child's ______ was to be king.
