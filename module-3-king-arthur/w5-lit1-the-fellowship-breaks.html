📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A great kingdom rarely falls in one blow. It comes apart one choice at a time.
The Round Table does not collapse because an enemy army is stronger. It comes apart from the inside. Today you trace cause and effect: how a private fault and a few accidents grow into civil war. The craft term to hold onto is conflict, the struggle that drives a story. Here the deepest conflict is not knight against monster. It is loyalty pulled in two directions at once.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to trace the chain of cause and effect that breaks the fellowship of the Round Table.
2
I will be able to explain how a knight's loyalty can pull him in two directions at once.

🏛️ A LITTLE CONTEXT FIRST
In the old legends, a knight swore loyalty to several things at once: to his king, to his fellow knights, to the lady he served, and to the code of chivalry itself. As long as those loyalties pointed the same way, a knight could be perfect. Green has spent the whole book calling Arthur's kingdom the Realm of Logres, his name for the model of chivalry standing against barbarism. In this part of the book those loyalties finally point in different directions, and Green writes a single hard sentence about the result: "The realm of Logres was broken." Keep that idea of competing loyalties in mind as you read.
📚 THE LESSON

Cause and effect is the chain of this happened, so that happened that holds a story together. Watch how tightly Green links the events here. Launcelot's love for Queen Guinevere is exposed, so she is condemned to be burned. Launcelot rides in to rescue her from the stake, but in the chaos he strikes down the unarmed Gareth and Gaheris, two of Gawain's brothers, without even knowing it. That accident turns Gawain, once Launcelot's friend, into an unforgiving enemy, so Britain splits into civil war. Then, while Arthur is away fighting Launcelot, his treacherous nephew Mordred commits treason and seizes the kingdom for himself. Notice what kind of conflict this is. No villain marches in from outside to destroy Logres. The wound is self-inflicted, and the most painful blows fall between people who were on the same side. That is why this part of the story is a tragedy: good people, pulled between loyalties they cannot all keep, make choices that destroy what they most wanted to protect. Your job today is to follow the chain link by link and notice where each person's loyalty splits.
