The Real Test: Honor When No One Is Watching
Literature · Week 2
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The hardest fight in a story is sometimes inside one person.
Gawain's real test is not the axe. It is three quiet temptations and one small lie about a magic green girdle. A struggle between a character and an outside force is external conflict; a struggle inside a character's own heart is internal conflict. Today you watch both, and see why one small lie costs Gawain a nick on the neck.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to tell internal conflict from external conflict in Gawain's story.
2
I will be able to explain why the nick on Gawain's neck is a fair result of his one hidden lie.

📚 THE LESSON

Conflict is the struggle that drives a story. External conflict is a character against an outside force: Gawain against the Green Knight and his deadly bargain. Internal conflict is a struggle inside a character: Gawain's fear of death pulling against his promise to be honest. On the road Gawain reaches a castle whose lord makes a friendly bargain: each day they will trade whatever each has won. For two days Gawain courteously refuses the lord's wife and honestly hands over what he received. On the third day she gives him a green girdle she says will keep its wearer from being slain, and Gawain, afraid of the blow he owes, accepts it and hides it from his host. That single concealed lie is his one fault. At the Green Chapel the Green Knight, revealed as the castle's lord, feints twice and gives only a small nick on the third blow, punishment for the lie, mercy for everything Gawain did right. Your job today is to separate the outside danger from the struggle inside Gawain.
