📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
An author can plant small signs early that point to what is coming.
In the village, a band of boys gathers around Daniel, using the bronze bow as their password. Meanwhile Thacia gently befriends Leah, and Leah begs to hear, again and again, the story of a girl Jesus raised from death. When an author plants a hint that prepares you for a later event, the craft term is foreshadowing. Today you'll learn to spot the quiet signs Speare is planting now.

⚡ WARM-UP · TAP TO REVEAL
Leah begs to hear the same story over and over: a girl Jesus raised from death. Why might Speare repeat it? Guess, then tap.
A frightened, shut-in girl keeps asking to hear one particular story of healing. What could that be preparing the reader for? (Make a guess, then tap.)
It is a quiet hint, a piece of foreshadowing. Leah's fascination with a story of healing prepares the reader, gently, for the possibility that Leah herself might one day be healed. Speare plants the idea long before it could happen.
📚 THE LESSON

Foreshadowing is when an author plants an early hint that prepares the reader for something later. A theme is the deeper idea a book keeps returning to. In these chapters both grow at once. A band of village boys gathers around Daniel, using the bronze bow as their secret password, so Daniel's cause begins to look like a real group. At the same time, Thacia quietly befriends Leah, bringing small gifts and drawing the frightened girl a little way out of her shell. Leah keeps begging to hear one story: a girl whom Jesus raised from death. That repeated story is foreshadowing. It plants the idea of healing in the reader's mind long before any healing happens. Notice the two threads pulling in opposite directions: the band points toward fighting Rome, while Leah's slow thaw points toward the power of patient love. Both feed the book's theme, the question of what truly sets a person free, force or love. As you read, watch for one small detail that seems to hint at something still to come.
