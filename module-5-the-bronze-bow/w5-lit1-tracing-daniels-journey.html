Tracing Daniel's Journey

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A good essay starts before you write. It starts by laying the evidence out in order.
No new chapters this week. Your essay argues how Daniel changes across the whole book, so today you gather the proof. You will lay out five moments, in order, that mark his journey from hatred and vengeance toward love. The craft work for the week is tracing a theme across an arc: showing how an idea grows from the first page to the last. Each moment you sort today becomes evidence in your essay.

📚 THE LESSON

A theme is the idea a story argues across its whole length, and the best way to prove a theme is to trace it: lay out the moments where the idea changes, in the order they happen. Daniel begins the book ruled by hatred. After the Romans crucify his father and uncle, he makes a vow at age eight to hate them, fight them, and kill them, and for years that vow is his whole identity. Then the book moves him, step by step. He spares the old miser instead of robbing him. He takes in his sister Leah and accepts Simon's shop so he can care for her. He breaks with Rosh when Rosh will not lift a finger to save Joel, telling him, "I am not one of your men. Not any longer." He hears Jesus say that hate, not Rome, is the real enemy, and that the only thing stronger than hate is love. He hits a low point when his fight has cost Samson and Nathan their lives and he feels he has nothing left but his hatred. And at the very end, with Leah healed and the weight of hatred gone, he crosses the road to the Roman soldier Marcus and asks, "Will you come in to our house?" That is the arc your essay will trace. Today you put these moments in order and mark the chapter for each, so your proof is ready before you write a single paragraph.

💡 TRY THIS · WORD WORK
Write one sentence describing Daniel at the start of the book and one describing him at the end. Use the word vow in both. Then add one line: which single moment, in your view, turned the first Daniel into the second?
