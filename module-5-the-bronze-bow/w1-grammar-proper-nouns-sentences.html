Lesson 5.04
Naming Names and Shaping Sentences
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 1
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A capital letter and an end mark each carry meaning of their own.
A book full of real-sounding places and people, like Galilee, Capernaum, and Daniel, runs on proper nouns. You'll learn to spot a proper noun and capitalize it, then learn the four sentence types and the end marks they take.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to tell a common noun from a proper noun and capitalize proper nouns correctly.
2
I will be able to name the four sentence types and choose the right end mark for each.
⚡ WARM-UP
Quick gut check: which of these would you write with a capital letter? "the sea of galilee" (No wrong answer, just check in.)
📚 THE LESSON

A common noun names any one of a kind: a boy, a village, a sea. A proper noun names one specific person, place, or thing, and it is always capitalized: Daniel, Capernaum, the Sea of Galilee. The Bronze Bow is full of proper nouns, because Speare sets her story in a real place and time. Getting the capitals right is part of writing about it clearly. Sentences also come in four types, and each does a different job. A declarative sentence makes a statement and ends in a period. An interrogative sentence asks a question and ends in a question mark. An imperative sentence gives a command or request and usually ends in a period. An exclamatory sentence shows strong feeling and ends in an exclamation point. When you write about Daniel this week, mixing these four types keeps your writing from sounding flat, and matching the right end mark keeps it clear.

Sentence typeIts jobEnd markExample
Declarativemakes a statement.Daniel lives on the mountain.
Interrogativeasks a question?Why does Daniel hate Rome?
Imperativegives a command or request.Read the next chapter.
Exclamatoryshows strong feeling!The Romans took everything!
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Reopen Chapters 1–3 and find three proper nouns Speare uses, such as a person's name or a place name. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Write them, correctly capitalized, in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Week-1 Reading Journal lesson). Then write one declarative, one interrogative, and one exclamatory sentence about Chapter 1.

💪 BEFORE YOU GO

From now on, capitalize every name of a real person or place, and ask what each sentence is doing before you choose its end mark. Small marks, clear meaning.
