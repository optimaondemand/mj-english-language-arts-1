Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 2
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A word read in its own sentence is a word you can keep.
This week's words come from Chapters 9–16: money and trade, the smithy, and a kind of teaching story Jesus uses. You'll meet each one where it lives in the book and use the surrounding sentence, the context clues, to find its meaning. Then you'll make one word your own by rewriting a sentence three ways.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's words correctly in the context of the story.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP · TAP TO REVEAL
Guess first: what is a "parable," the kind of story Jesus tells? Then tap the card.
Jesus teaches with short stories about everyday things, like a good neighbor or a lost coin. What do we call that kind of story? (Make a guess, then tap.)
A parable is a short, simple story that teaches a lesson or a deeper truth. The everyday details, a neighbor, a coin, a seed, carry a meaning larger than themselves, much like a symbol does.
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Every word below belongs to Chapters 9–16. Some name money and trade in the village. Others name Daniel's work at the smithy or the kind of story Jesus tells. Watch for them as you read.

WordWhat it means here
misera person who hoards money and hates to spend it (the old man Daniel is sent to rob)
talismanan object kept for luck or protection (the special dagger Rosh has carried for years)
recompensepayment or reward for work or trouble (the coins Leah had never been paid for her weaving)
dowrymoney or goods a bride brings to a marriage (the coins that would be Leah's dowry)
parablea short, simple story that teaches a deeper truth (the stories Jesus tells)
anvila heavy iron block a blacksmith hammers metal against (Daniel beats out his anger on it)
bellowsa tool pumped to blow air and make a fire burn hotter at the forge
looma frame for weaving cloth from thread (Leah works for hours at the loom)
consciencethe inner sense of right and wrong that troubles Daniel after the robbery
creeda set of beliefs a person lives by (Rosh's creed: take money where you find it)
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Week-1 Reading Journal lesson):
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Hunt for two of this week's words inside Chapters 9–16. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Copy each full sentence into the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them becomes the sentence you rewrite three ways above.
⚡ TRY IT · THE RIGHT WORD
Choose the word that fits each sentence from the world of the novel.

2. Jesus taught the crowd with a ______ about a kind traveler who helped a stranger.
3. At the forge, Daniel struck the hot iron against the ______, blow after blow.
