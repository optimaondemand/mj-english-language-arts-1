Lesson 5.06
Two Voices Inside One Boy
Literature · Week 2
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A character's choices, more than the author's words, show who they really are.
Rosh sends Daniel to rob an old miser. Daniel knocks the man down, then spares him and gives back his dagger. In that one choice, two voices fight inside Daniel: Rosh's "take the money where we find it," and the carpenter's "each one of you is precious in His sight." This is Daniel's internal conflict growing louder, and it tells you what he wants more clearly than any description could.

1
I will be able to name the two opposing "voices" pulling at Daniel in Chapter 9.
2
I will be able to explain how Daniel's choice to spare the miser reveals his character.

📚 THE LESSON

One of the strongest tools an author has is to reveal character through choice. Speare rarely stops to tell you what Daniel is like. She puts him in a hard spot and lets his decision speak. In Chapter 9, Rosh sends Daniel to rob an old miser. Daniel knocks the man down, but the old man reminds him of his own grandfather, and Daniel cannot finish the robbery. He spares him and even gives back the man's dagger. Afterward, Rosh names a "soft streak" in Daniel and vows to hammer it out, the way a smith hammers a flaw from metal. But the carpenter's words keep echoing in Daniel's mind: "each one of you is precious in His sight." Two voices now pull at him. Rosh's voice says people are tools for the cause. The other voice says every person matters. This is Daniel's internal conflict, and his choice to spare the miser shows that the second voice is starting to win, even though Daniel himself does not yet understand the change. As you read, watch for the exact moment Daniel decides.
