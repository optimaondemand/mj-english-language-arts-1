Lesson 5.09
Verb Tenses: Telling When Things Happen
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 2
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The form of a verb tells your reader when something happens.
When you write about a book, you choose a tense and stick to it. You'll review past, present, and future tense, then practice the skill that trips up real writers: keeping one tense steady so your reader is never confused. A short quiz checks it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name a verb's tense as past, present, or future.
2
I will be able to fix a sentence that shifts tense by mistake.
⚡ WARM-UP
Quick gut check: in "Daniel walks to the village and spared the miser," does something feel off? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THE LESSON

A verb's tense tells when its action happens. Past tense shows an action that already happened: Daniel spared the miser. Present tense shows an action happening now or that is generally true: Daniel works at the smithy. Future tense shows an action that has not happened yet and uses the helping word will: Daniel will lead the band. The trick when you write about a story is to pick one tense and hold it. A tense shift happens when a writer jumps from one tense to another by mistake, like "Daniel walks to the village and spared the miser." That jump confuses the reader, because the action seems to be both happening now and already over. To fix it, choose one tense for the whole sentence: "Daniel walked to the village and spared the miser," or "Daniel walks to the village and spares the miser." When you write about Daniel this week, pick a tense up front and stay in it.

TenseWhen the action happensExample
Pastalready happenedDaniel spared the miser.
Presenthappening now or generally trueDaniel works at the smithy.
