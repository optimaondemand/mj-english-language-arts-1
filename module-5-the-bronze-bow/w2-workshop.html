📚 STOP 5 · THE WORLD OF THE BOOK · TAKE NOTES WITHOUT COPYING

Daniel's Galilee was a real province under Roman rule, and this week you go read about it. You already know how to find a source, skim it, read its features, and name its central idea. Here is the habit that makes all of that safe to use. When you copy a writer's sentence into your notes and later hand it in as though you wrote it, that is plagiarism. It counts even when it was an accident, and an accident is usually how it happens: a sentence gets pasted into a document, the quotation marks fall off, and two weeks later nobody remembers whose words those were. The fix is not a rule to memorize. It is a physical habit. Read the paragraph, then look away from the screen, and write what it said in your own words. If you cannot write it without looking, you have not understood it yet, so read it again. Putting an idea in your own words is called paraphrasing, and it is the single most useful note-taking skill you will learn this year. When you genuinely want a writer's exact words, keep them, but put quotation marks around them and write down where they came from, right there in your notes. Later, that note is the difference between a citation and a problem.
