Lesson 5.20
The Bronze Bow: A Symbol That Changes Its Meaning
Literature · Week 4
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The same image can mean one thing at the start of a book and the opposite by the end.
The novel takes its title from an old song about a bow of bronze that no ordinary person could bend. For most of the book Daniel reads that image as a call to war. By Chapter 24 the same image has come to mean something else. When an object stands for an idea larger than itself, the craft term is symbol. Today you trace how Speare lets one symbol shift its meaning as Daniel changes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what the symbol of the bronze bow means at the start and at the end of the novel.
2
I will be able to connect the bow that "no man can bend by his own strength" to Daniel's change.

⚡ WARM-UP · CHECK IN FIRST

📜 THE SONG BEHIND THE TITLE
The band's password and the book's title come from an old song of David, found in the Bible at Psalm 18 and 2 Samuel 22. One line runs:
"He trains my hands for war, so that my arms can bend a bow of bronze."
Read it closely. The person does not bend the bronze bow by their own muscle. Their hands are trained and strengthened by someone else. That small detail is the key to the title.
📚 THE LESSON

A symbol is an object that stands for an idea larger than itself, and a skilled author can let a symbol shift meaning across a story. The bronze bow does exactly that. Early in the novel Daniel and his friends adopt the bow as the sign of their fight against Rome. To Daniel it means strength, war, and victory by force. But the song it comes from says the bow is bent only because God trains and strengthens the hand. A bow of bronze is too stiff for any person to bend alone, so the image is really about doing something impossible with strength that is not your own. By the end of the book, the impossible thing Daniel bends is not a weapon. It is his own hatred. He could not soften it by willpower for the whole novel. Only after Jesus comes does the hardened thing finally bend. So the symbol that began as a call to war ends as a picture of a heart changed by love. Your job today is to state both meanings of the bow and to connect "a bow no man can bend alone" to the change in Daniel.
