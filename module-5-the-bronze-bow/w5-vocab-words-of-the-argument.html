Lesson 5.29
The Words of the Argument
Grammar & Vocabulary · Week 5
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Your essay is about a change of heart, so you need the words that name that change and the words that name an argument. This week's terms come from The Bronze Bow and from the craft of persuasion. You'll meet each one in a real sentence, then make one your own by rewriting it three ways. Reading a word inside a sentence lets you use the surrounding words to figure out its meaning. The craft term for those surrounding hints is context clues.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use this week's argument and theme words correctly when I write about Daniel.
2
I will be able to take one sentence and rewrite it three ways to make a word my own.
⚡ WARM-UP
Some of this week's words name an argument; others name what changes inside Daniel. Which kind feels harder to use precisely? (No wrong answer.)
📚 THIS WEEK'S WORDS, WHERE THEY LIVE

Half of these words name parts of an argument, the kind you are building in your essay. The other half name the change of heart Daniel goes through. Watch how each one fits the work of writing about The Bronze Bow.

WordWhat it means here
thesisthe main claim of an essay; the one debatable point you set out to prove (your essay's thesis about Daniel)
counterargumentthe strongest case against your claim, which you state fairly and then answer
persuadeto move someone to believe or do something by reasons, character, or feeling (Daniel persuades Joel to swear the oath)
evidencethe facts and details from the text that support a claim
vengeanceharm done in return for a wrong; revenge (Daniel's vow is built on vengeance for his father)
transformationa complete change in someone or something (Daniel's transformation from hatred to love)
convictiona firm, deeply held belief (Daniel's conviction that only the sword could free Israel)
reconcileto make peace; to restore a friendly relationship after conflict (Daniel reconciles with the enemy he hated)
vowa solemn promise (Daniel's vow to hate, fight, and kill the Romans)
reprisalan act of harm in return for an attack; retaliation (the Romans threaten reprisal against the village)
✏️ THE WRITTEN PRACTICE · MAKE A WORD YOUR OWN
Pick one word from the table above. Find (or write) one sentence that uses it. Then rewrite that sentence three ways in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal (the Word doc you set up in the Week-1 Reading Journal lesson):
  1. Swap: replace the word with a synonym, a word that means almost the same thing. Does the meaning shift?
  2. Stretch: keep the word but add detail until the picture is sharper.
  3. Imitate: keep the sentence's shape but write about something in your own life.
Finish with one line: which version is strongest, and why?
📖 FROM THE BOOK
Find one place in The Bronze Bow that shows Daniel's vengeance and one that shows his transformation. (Read from your own copy of the book.) Write a sentence about each in the "Week-by-Week Notes" section of your Reading Journal. One of them can become the sentence you rewrite three ways above, and both can become evidence in your essay.
⚡ TRY IT · THE RIGHT WORD
Choose the word that fits each sentence about the book or your essay.
1. The first paragraph of your essay states your ______: the one claim you will prove about Daniel.
2. Daniel's vow to hurt the Romans for killing his father is an act of ______.
3. By the last chapter, Daniel has undergone a full ______ from hatred to love.
4. After your thesis, you state the ______, the best case against your reading, and then answer it.
