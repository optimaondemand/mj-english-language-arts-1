✏️ Grammar: Learn
The phrase, its comma, and its person.

A participle is a verb form, usually ending in -ing or -ed, that describes a noun. Add its words and you have a participial phrase. A phrase that opens a sentence is followed by a comma. The phrase must describe the person right after the comma, or it dangles.

WordWhat it doesExample
participlea verb form, often -ing or -ed, that describes a nounrummaging, dazed
participial phrasethe participle plus its wordsrummaging in his shop
opening phrasea comma follows itRummaging in his shop, he found a bit of broken pottery.
closing phrasecan follow a comma at the endJoktan sat outside the shop, halfheartedly filing down a set of nails.
dangling phrasedescribes the wrong nounLaying aside the leeches, the house was quiet.
periodic sentenceholds the main idea, who does what, until the endFramed in the doorway, against the sunlight, stood Jesus.
loose sentencesays the main idea firstJesus stood in the doorway, framed against the sunlight.

Noor’s draft

A. In The Bronze Bow, Elizabeth George Speare shows that love can do what hatred cannot, because Daniel’s hatred only traps him, while love sets him free.

B. In Ch. 21, Jesus tells Daniel that hate is the enemy, not men, and that the only thing stronger than hate is love. This supports my thesis because the book states its answer before Daniel can live it. In Ch. 23, after Simon says Jesus will never lead them against Rome, Daniel is left with nothing but his hatred and his vow. This supports my thesis because hatred leaves him with no friend to fight beside him and no leader to follow. Crossing the road in Ch. 24, Daniel asks the soldier, “Will you come in to our house?” This supports my thesis because he does the one thing his hatred would never let him do.

C. Someone could argue that hatred was useful, because it gave Daniel a purpose when he had nothing else; even in Ch. 24, purpose “remained to him” after Leah had lost everything.
