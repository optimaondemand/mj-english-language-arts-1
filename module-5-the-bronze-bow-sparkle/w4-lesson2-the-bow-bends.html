Professor Lovelore

Yes. In the first part of the chapter, Daniel thought God did not mean the bow of bronze for him. Now he wonders if only love could bend it. The bow has paid off. And notice how quiet it is. No speech, no crowd. The weight simply goes. That is the story’s climax, the high point where everything turns, and Speare makes it still.

Noor’s plan
6th Grade Scholar

Thesis: Love does what Daniel’s hatred never could, because hatred only traps him and love sets him free.

Moment 1: Ch. 21. Jesus tells Daniel the only thing stronger than hate is love. Why: the book says its answer out loud before Daniel can live it.

Moment 2: Ch. 23. After Simon’s words, Daniel has nothing left but his hatred and his vow. Why: hatred leaves him with no friend and no leader.

Moment 3: Ch. 24. Daniel asks the soldier, “Will you come in to our house?” Why: he does the one thing his hatred would never let him do.

Objection: Hatred gave Daniel a purpose. Even in Ch. 24, purpose “remained to him” when Leah had lost everything.
