The three ways a speaker tries to win someone over.
Logos
reasons and facts that lead to a conclusion
Ethos
trust in who the speaker is
Pathos
feelings strong enough to move someone

Imani’s other side
6th Grade Scholar

It is true that hatred kept Daniel going. After his father died, his vow became all he lived for (Ch. 7). Even in the last chapter, when Leah has lost everything, “purpose remained to him” (Ch. 24). A careful reader could argue that without his hatred, Daniel would have given up long before the end.

Imani’s answer
6th Grade Scholar

Yet the same hatred that kept Daniel going also held him prisoner. After the rescue, he sees that Samson’s and Nathan’s deaths are “on his head,” and freedom is farther away than before (Ch. 20). In the last chapter his hatred is “as strong as ever,” and he is “like a man imprisoned in a pit” (Ch. 24). Only when the terrible weight is gone does he feel “a peace he had never imagined” (Ch. 24). Hatred gave Daniel a purpose, but love gave him back his life.
