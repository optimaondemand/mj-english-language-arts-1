🍎 At the Board with Professor Lovelore
Parallel structure, colons and semicolons
Professor Lovelore At the board
1:20
An AI-generated character and voice.
1. Parallel structure
every item in a list has the same shape
Daniel works the forge, cares for Leah, and meets with the band.
2. The colon ( : )
a full sentence first, then a list or an explanation
Daniel hears three voices: Rosh, Hezron, and the teacher.
3. The semicolon ( ; )
joins two full sentences that belong together: no and, no but
Rosh trusts the sword; the teacher trusts love.
Periodic sentence (PS)
the main idea waits until the end
After the vow, after the mountain, Daniel crosses the road.
Participial phrase (PP)
describes the person right beside it
Standing in the doorway, Daniel looked across the road.

✏️ Grammar: Learn
Tap the parts.
MoveWhat it doesExample
Parallel structurekeeps the items in a list or a pair in the same formDaniel works the forge, cares for Leah, and meets with the band.
Colon ( : )introduces a list, an example or an explanation; a complete sentence comes before itDaniel hears three voices: Rosh, Hezron, and the teacher from Nazareth.
Semicolon ( ; )joins two complete sentences that belong together, with no and, but or becauseRosh looks at a man and sees a tool; Jesus looks and sees a child of God.
Periodic sentence (PS)holds the main idea back until the endAfter the vow, after the mountain, after the long days beside Leah’s bed, Daniel crosses the road.
Participial phrase (PP)an -ing or -ed word group that describes the noun right beside itSwept up by the boy’s passion, Daniel was checked by a sudden stab of guilt.
Self-editread your own sentence for one move at a timeIs every list parallel? Is there a full sentence before each colon, and on both sides of each semicolon? Does each PP sit next to the person it describes?

Imani’s central argument
6th Grade Scholar

Rosh and the teacher look at the same people in two different ways. Parallel Rosh looks at a man and sees a tool; Jesus looks and sees a child of God (Ch. 9). When Daniel carries an old man he has robbed into the shade and gives back one of his daggers, Rosh calls it “a soft streak” that must be hammered out (Ch. 9). PP Standing before Rosh at the mouth of the cave, Daniel finally sees “a man he had never really looked at before” (Ch. 18). PS After the rescue, after the loss of Samson and Nathan, after days of guilt, Daniel hears the teacher say that hate, not men, is the enemy (Ch. 21). Each moment moves Daniel one step further from Rosh’s way. The Wordburden he carries is not Rome; it is his own hatred.
