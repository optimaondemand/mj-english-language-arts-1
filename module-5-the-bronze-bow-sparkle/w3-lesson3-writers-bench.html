✏️ Grammar: Learn
Step 1 of 2 this week: adjectives and appositives.

An adjective describes a noun. An appositive is a noun or noun phrase placed right beside another noun to rename it. When it adds extra information, commas set it off.

WordWhat it doesExample
adjectivedescribes a noun: which kind, how many, what likea hard mouth, a white veil
appositivea noun or noun phrase beside another noun that renames itRosh, the outlaw leader, said it was not his affair.
commasset off an appositive that adds extra information: one before it and one after it (a comma after it if it opens the sentence, before it if it closes the sentence)Joel, the scribe’s son, came to the shop.
drop testtake the appositive out, and the sentence still worksJoel came to the shop.
why use oneintroduces a character in one tight sentence, so an essay can name many characters at onceNathan, a new bridegroom, ...

Sofia’s paragraph

The sword cost Daniel more than he ever planned to pay. To free Joel, Daniel led the boys of the band against a Roman column on the Via Maris, and the rescue worked. But Nathan, a new bridegroom, was killed, and Samson, the big man who had followed the boys all night, was taken by the soldiers (Ch. 19). Afterward, in the autumn rain, Daniel could not stop hearing the words “They who live by the sword will perish by the sword” (Ch. 20). In Ch. 21 the teacher told him, “The only thing stronger than hate is love.” Daniel could not accept it yet, and by the end of Ch. 23 he had nothing left but his hatred and his vow. Speare shows that the sword did not bring freedom closer; it took away the people Daniel loved.

Underlined: the appositives.
