🖋️ Pen, step 1Tap the three stages the grain grows through.
4:26 And he said, So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed into the ground; 4:27 And should sleep, and rise night and day, and the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how. 4:28 For the earth bringeth forth fruit of herself; first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn in the ear. 4:29 But when the fruit is brought forth, immediately he putteth in the sickle, because the harvest is come. 4:30 And he said, Whereunto shall we liken the kingdom of God? or with what comparison shall we compare it? 4:31 It is like a grain of mustard seed, which, when it is sown in the earth, is less than all the seeds that be in the earth: 4:32 But when it is sown, it groweth up, and becometh greater than all herbs, and shooteth out great branches; so that the fowls of the air may lodge under the shadow of it.
kingdom of God: God’s rule among people; the teacher explains it by comparisons (parables) · blade: the first green shoot · ear: the head of grain on the stalk · corn: grain (wheat or barley) · sickle: a curved blade for cutting grain · liken: compare · mustard seed: a very tiny seed · herbs: garden plants · fowls: birds · lodge: rest, nest
Which person in Chapters 13 to 16 grows like this seed, a little at a time?

🔤 Words: Chapters 13–16
Tap a card to hear it.
recompense
Payment or reward for work or trouble.
“He realized that she had never before known any recompense for her hours at the loom.” Speare, Ch. 13
rebellion
A fight against whatever holds you in control.
“Without warning, Leah’s childish questions had unleashed all the rebellion he had kept so carefully chained.” Speare, Ch. 14
misgiving
A feeling of doubt or worry that something may go wrong.
“Then, watching Joel’s rapt flushed face, Daniel felt not envy, but unexpected misgiving.” Speare, Ch. 16
intrigue
Secret planning and plotting.
“Joel’s eyes began to sparkle at the thought of intrigue.” Speare, Ch. 16
ambition
A strong wish to reach a goal or become something.
“It was the first time he had ever voiced his ambition, even to himself.” Speare, Ch. 16

Also in these chapters:

dowry
Coins or goods a bride brings to her marriage.
“…every village girl, even the poorest, boasted the jingling coins that would be her dowry.” Speare, Ch. 13
talent
Here, a silver coin.
“When Daniel had laid the shining silver talent in his sister’s hand, she had been bewildered.” Speare, Ch. 13
Samaritan
A person from Samaria, a people many Galileans scorned.
“…a cursed Samaritan stopped and bound up his wounds and took care of him.” Speare, Ch. 15
milestone
A stone marking each mile on a Roman road.
“Finally they reached the milestone.” Speare, Ch. 16
