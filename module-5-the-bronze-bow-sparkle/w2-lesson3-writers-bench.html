✏️ Grammar: Learn
Step 1 of 2: verbs and tense.

A verb shows an action or a way of being. The tense of a verb tells when: it already happened, it is happening now, or it has not happened yet.

WordWhat it doesExample
verbshows action or beingcarried, wove, was
past tensealready happenedDaniel carried the water jars.
present tensehappening now or generally trueDaniel works at Simon’s forge.
future tensehas not happened yet; uses willJoel will sell fish to the banker’s servants.
tense shifta jump from one tense to another by mistakeDaniel took the bag and turns away.
steady tenseone tense held all through a passageLeah sank down beside him and listened.
strong verban exact verb in place of went, got, did, had, or wasslammed, flung, wheeled

Levi’s paragraph

In Chapter 9, two voices pulled at Daniel. Rosh told him, “We take the money where we find it,” and he asked what loss one old man would be. Daniel took the miser’s money, but when he looked back and saw the old man lying in the road, he went back, carried him into the shade, and waited until he woke. Later, at the anvil, he remembered the carpenter’s words: “For each one of you is precious in His sight.” The same pull showed in Chapter 14, when Daniel feasted on the mountain but lay awake wondering if a village child would go hungry because of the feast. At dawn he walked back down to Leah. Rosh’s voice was still loud, but the carpenter’s words had begun to change what Daniel did.
