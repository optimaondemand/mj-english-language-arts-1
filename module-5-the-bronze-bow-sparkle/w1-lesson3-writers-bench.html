✏️ Grammar: Learn
Names first, then sentences.

A common noun names any one of a kind. A proper noun names one particular person, place, or thing, and it always takes a capital letter. Every sentence also has a job, and its end mark follows the job.

WordWhat it doesExample
common nounnames any one of a kindboy, village, lake
proper nounnames one particular person, place, or thing; takes a capitalDaniel, Ketzah, the Sabbath
declarativetells; ends with a periodDaniel lives on the mountain.
interrogativeasks; ends with a question markIs that you, Daniel?
imperativetells someone to do something; usually ends with a periodGet rid of them.
exclamatorycries out with strong feeling; ends with an exclamation pointHow blue the lake is!

Amara’s paragraph
6th Grade Scholar

Daniel’s hatred of Rome is built on one terrible time. When he was eight, Roman soldiers crucified his father and his uncle, and his mother died a few weeks later (Ch. 7). Daniel vowed to pay them back with his whole life. What does he want most? On the roof of his grandmother’s house in Ketzah, he pictures the Romans gone, a good house for his grandmother and Leah, and everyone walking free (Ch. 4). His hatred has done something real for him: it gave a starving runaway on the mountain something to live for (Ch. 1), and it keeps him hammering out swords for Rosh’s band (Ch. 5). To Daniel, hatred is not a weakness but the reason he gets up each day.
