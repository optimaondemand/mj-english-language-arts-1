Big Question
Today’s Wonder

A stranger asks a king for almost nothing and will not give his name. What could a person gain by asking for less?

✏️ Daily Oral Language
Study the model, then fix the sentence.
✅ Correct model
Sir Gawain spoke kindly to the stranger at Camelot.

Names of particular people and places start with a capital letter: Sir Gawain, Camelot. A title is part of the name when it comes right before it. A word that tells how someone spoke is an adverb: kindly, not kind.

Gareth and Lynette
Alfred, Lord Tennyson (1872), from Idylls of the King
Tennyson spells her Lynette and her sister Lyonors; our book says Linnet and Liones.
Last, Gareth leaning both hands heavily Down on the shoulders of the twain, his men, Approached between them toward the King, and asked, “A boon, Sir King (his voice was all ashamed), For see ye not how weak and hungerworn I seem—leaning on these? grant me to serve For meat and drink among thy kitchen-knaves A twelvemonth and a day, nor seek my name. Hereafter I will fight.” To him the King, “A goodly youth and worth a goodlier boon! But so thou wilt no goodlier, then must Kay, The master of the meats and drinks, be thine.”
boon: a favour asked of a king · twain: two · hungerworn: thin and weak from hunger · kitchen-knaves: kitchen servants · twelvemonth: a year · hereafter: later on · goodly: fine, handsome · goodlier: better · so thou wilt no goodlier: if you will not ask for anything better · the master of the meats and drinks: Sir Kay, who runs the King’s household and kitchen
